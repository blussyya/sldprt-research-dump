# EXP-089: face-attached sketch bodies and component tails

This experiment follows two open fields from EXP-088: the one-face bodies embedded in
`ResolvedFeatures`, and the six unnamed words after the ghost-body ID in a component reference.
It also records the first corpus map of the opaque dimension-placement ranges.

Scripts, generated rows, and independent checks are under `scripts/EXP089/`.

## ResolvedFeatures stores the selected support face with a face-attached sketch

NQ-048 began with 34 valid one-plane-face sections in the original files. Adding the SW2011 and
SW2022 C18 controls gives 36. The feature reader reaches 13 of those owners. Every one is an
`sgSketch` with `onPlane=0`, and the stored compressed bytes match the separately parsed Parasolid
section exactly.

The direct mappings are:

- C04, C05, C06 and C11: the section belongs to Sketch2.
- C07 and C08: the two sections belong to Sketch2 and Sketch3.
- SW2011 and SW2022 C18: the section belongs to Sketch2.
- Helical Bevel Gear: the sections belong to Sketch2, Sketch9 and Sketch10.

The controlled geometry settles the role. C04's embedded body is the complete top face before the
hole: a plane at z = 0.01 m with one 10 x 10 mm square loop and four edges. The finished top face has
that square plus a circular inner loop, while Sketch2 contains only the circle. In C07, Sketch2
stores the untouched top face; Sketch3 stores the same face after the first hole, with two loops and
five edges. The bodies are snapshots of the selected support face at the point when each sketch was
made. That explains why the count follows later cuts and split lines.

The main survey finds 40 such sections in the original, C18, and later holefillets copies. Fourteen
map directly to decoded sketch objects; the other 26 lie after the current feature reader stops. A
blind scan reproduced the 36-section NQ set and the controlled mappings.

The blind scan also found a limit that matters. Across the online corpus, valid one-face sections
can belong to other object types. One concrete counterexample is a section in
`51065319_Mirror (opt 1-PSA)-HME_D.SLDPRT` beside `moSingleFaceRef_w`. Its following word is
`01000802`, unlike the zero word in the controlled sketches. The common size-plus-five and zero-flag
prefix is generic section framing and cannot identify `sgSketch` on its own.

Evidence: `scripts/EXP089/resolved-features/` and
`scripts/EXP089/resolved-features-blind/`.

## Six component-reference words are almost always zero

EXP-088 identified the first u32 of the 28-byte `@comp.x0` block as the document-local ghost-body
join ID. This survey checks the remaining six words without assigning meanings to zero fields.

The current feature reader recovers 863 unique component blocks from 205 feature streams among 222
part paths. Every block matches the raw feature stream at component object offset +17. The six words
after the ghost ID are zero in 862 blocks.

The only exception is `moCompFace_c` inside the helical gear's Plane1 feature:

```
[0, 0, 0, 0, 1, 0, 0]
```

The one is at byte 16 within `x0`. Thirty other `moCompFace_c` blocks have zero there. One example
cannot distinguish a face, plane, ownership, state, or version flag, so the field remains unnamed.
The safe description is that the remaining 24 bytes are zero or reserved in the observed corpus,
with one unexplained production face-reference flag. A blind probe independently recovered the
same 863 raw blocks and the same sole exception.

Evidence: `scripts/EXP089/component-block/` and
`scripts/EXP089/component-block-blind/`.

## Dimension-placement ranges now have a corpus map

The feature reader still scans over the placement record between a displayed dimension and its
favorite handle. The new survey captures 100 ranges in 51 files, totalling 51,738 bytes. It sees 13
lengths from 308 to 587 bytes. Four common SW2011/SW2022 pairs grow by two bytes: 585 to 587, 539 to
541, 513 to 515, and 336 to 338.

This is coverage for the next decoder, not a field layout. The first differing byte is often byte 3
and varies again in production files, so the two-byte growth does not identify a length field. The
forward scan remains until a bounded parser handles every observed family and rejects truncation.

Evidence: `scripts/EXP089/dimension-placement/`.
