## 1. NQ-044 — the 132-byte record is a bounding box plus bounding sphere

`parser/v0.2` skips 132 bytes between Block3 and the forward metadata. EXP-060 established they
are not a fourth array. The layout is now decoded:

| offset | type | field |
|---|---|---|
| `+12`, `+20`, `+28` | `f64` × 3 | centre (x, y, z) |
| `+36`, `+44`, `+52` | `f64` × 3 | max corner |
| `+60`, `+68`, `+76` | `f64` × 3 | min corner |
| `+84` | `f64` | radius |

All values are in **metres**, consistent with the rest of DisplayLists.

### Why this is proven rather than fitted

The record is **internally self-consistent**, which does not depend on comparing against anything
external:

| check | result |
|---|---|
| `+12/+20/+28` equals the exact midpoint of the `+36…+76` pair | **142 / 142** |
| `+84` equals the corner distance \|max − centre\| | **142 / 142** |

Both at `< 1e-12` absolute. A wrong field assignment could not produce a centre that is the exact
midpoint of two other triples *and* a radius that is the exact corner distance of the same pair,
on every face of every model.

### It is the analytic bounding box, not the tessellated one

| check | result |
|---|---|
| record bbox **contains** the tessellation bbox | **140 / 142** |
| record bbox **equals** the tessellation bbox | **0 / 142** |
| worst containment violation | **1.341e-9 m** (1.3 nanometres) |

The record is always strictly larger than the mesh bounds and never smaller by more than
nanometres — and display coordinates are `float32` (INV, ~1e-9 m relative at these magnitudes),
so the two violations are rounding in the *vertices*, not in the record.

This is the first analytic geometry we have read out of DisplayLists. Everything until now has
been tessellation plus the surface tag and its parameters. A cylinder's mesh is inscribed in the
true cylinder; its bounding record is not.

Holds across every surface type:

| tag | faces | contains |
|---|---|---|
| 4001 plane | 111 | 109 |
| 4002 cylinder | 16 | 16 |
| 4003 cone | 1 | 1 |
| 4004 sphere | 1 | 1 |
| 4005 torus | 3 | 3 |
| 4006 B-surface | 4 | 4 |
| (no metadata) | 6 | 6 |

The two non-containing faces are planes in `C02_cube_translated`, at 1.3 nm — the float32 case
above, not a surface-type effect.

### Not yet decoded in this record

Bytes `+0…+11`, `+32`, `+56`, `+80`, and `+88…+131` are untouched. The first three words are
small integers (`0 0 1` on the cube's face 0) matching no count we know: the vertex, strip,
Block1 and Block3 counts there are 4, 1, 6 and 6. Tail words include `2147680259` and
`4294967295`, the latter a plausible sentinel.

## 2. Block2 is finished

> Is there anything left in Block2?

No. It is completely determined by the strip-length precursor.

| check | corpus | result |
|---|---|---|
| `Block2[i] != 2·L[i] − 2` | 11,515 strips | **0** |
| `Block2` count != strip count | 1,414 faces | **0** |
| distinct `Block2` header forms | 1,414 faces | **1** (`4, 8, 2`) |

Every word is a function of the precursor, the entry count is a function of the strip count, and
the header never varies. **Block2 carries zero information independent of data we already read.**

Corpus: 45 modern files — 21 under `test files original`, 24 under `test files new/SW2022` —
read directly from the stream rather than through the parser's own validation gate, so this is
not the gate confirming itself.

What remains is a *design* question, not a decode one: why store a redundant table at all.
Plausibly to let a reader seek Block1 section boundaries without first summing the precursor.
That is unfalsifiable from file contents and is recorded as an observation, not a finding.

**Block2 requires no further work.** INV-018 and INV-020 already state everything it holds.
