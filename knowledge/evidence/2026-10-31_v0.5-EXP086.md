# EXP-086 — Three of the five online parts that didn't resolve

**Date**: 2026-10-31. **Code**: solidworks-converters `tool`, d773ed8 and 55bcccb.
**Scripts and output**: `scripts/EXP086/`.

NQ-058 listed five online parts whose B-rep didn't resolve. Three now do, and the change helps two
parts that used to resolve with badly wrong curves.

## d2515121: a swept surface cut by a plane

Edge 21786 is a closed loop, the intersection of a SWEPT_SURF with a plane. The march round it
never got back to its start: the loop is a 0.3 × 0.7 mm oval and the step kept missing the stop
test (`ring.js`, `nq058.js`).

The swept surface's profile is a rational quadratic B-spline (a full ellipse, weights 1 and
√2/2), and it already lies in the cutting plane, z = −1.6 mm. The sweep direction is the plane's
normal (`sw.js`). A swept surface cut by any plane is the profile projected along the sweep onto
that plane. The projection is affine, so a rational B-spline maps to one with the same knots and
weights and projected control points. That's exact, and the converter now does it for any swept
surface and plane before it tries a march. Fit error on the part: 9.7e-12 m.

The volume still doesn't compute on this part: the seam split stops on face 3474 ("chain does not
end on a cut line"). That's NQ-057 territory.

## 3c4752f1: a rolling-ball blend that touches a plane at one point

Edge 1655 is a plane meeting a 2 mm rolling-ball blend (supports a cone and a cylinder). The
spine's distance to the plane reaches 2.000000001 mm at exactly one point, which is the edge's
start vertex 2466 (`sp.js`). So the true blend touches the plane there. The fitted B-spline copy
of the blend missed it by 3.1 µm, and the corrector found no common point.

`footOn` already handles a constant-radius blend exactly (a pipe round its spine). Intersections
now use the raw blend instead of the fitted one whenever the radius is constant. The part
resolves.

## 0739c559: the same cylinder twice, and a tolerant vertex

Edge 2277 is the contact curve of a blend with cylinder node 2494. The blend's supports are
cylinders 2513 and 2512, and 2512 has exactly the same radius, axis and point as 2494 (`bb.js`).
SolidWorks wrote the same surface twice. The support is now matched by geometry when the node
isn't one of the blend's own.

Next, edge 6076 (a trimmed ellipse) has vertex 6150 30.3 µm off the curve. That vertex has its own
tolerance, 48.2 µm, so it's within what the file allows. The check now accepts a vertex off its
curve by up to its own tolerance.

## What's left

- `2f121983`: blend 5791's spine is the intersection of two offsets of swept surfaces (one a swept
  circle, one a swept cubic). Its chart is exact (every point on both offsets to 1e-7 mm,
  `spA.js`), but it has 5 points over 28 mm and the curve bows away from the first 9 mm chord:
  corrected samples come out 1.4 mm apart. Splitting the chord at the curve point over its
  middle didn't help. The steps grew as the chord shrank towards the start (2.6 mm on a 1.1 mm
  chord), so the foot on the offset surfaces is jumping there, and the chart isn't the problem.
  Not committed.
- `82981f2a` (head_back), looked at on the second run (`hb.js`). The "interp: band" error is a
  symptom. Blend 10239 (r = 1 mm) has a spine that's the intersection of offset 10238 and blend
  10237, from an L limit to a T limit. Face 10183 on that blend starts at the T end (its vertex
  7660 is exactly 1 mm from it) and runs 4.7 mm past it along the spine's tangent. Every boundary
  point projects to the spine's last parameter, so the face's spine range is zero wide, the section
  parameters come out NaN, and the interpolation fails on them. The two offset surfaces touch at
  the T limit and the curve carries on past it. The fix is to follow the spine beyond the T limit
  (the chart stops there) instead of clamping to it. Not done yet. (This was wrong, see the next
  section.)

## head_back, third run

The diagnosis above was wrong. The face doesn't run past the T limit (`centres.js`). Its other two
vertices are exactly 1 mm from the spine at t = 0.151, well inside the chart. The face's spine range
was found by projecting each boundary point locally, starting from the first one, which is the
vertex at the T limit, t = 1. From there the local search sticks at the spine's end. The curve does
carry on past the limit (`past.js`), but it bends away and the face's vertices are 2.6 and 3.5 mm
from it, so that was the wrong branch to follow. The converter now takes the global projection
when it puts the point closer to the blend radius.

That got head_back to blend 6976 (r = 5 mm), whose spine stopped with "march did not reach the end"
(`hb2.js`, `b6976.js`). Its three chart points were exactly 14 mm from offset 6975, a 7 mm offset
of B-spline 3433 (`b6976c.js`). That's twice the offset: we had the surface on the wrong side. Both
the offset and its basis have sense `-`. `offside.js` checks every offset in the online sample that
bounds an intersection curve with a chart, and finds which side of the basis the chart points sit
on, measured along the basis's own du × dv normal:

| offset sense | basis sense | chart points on | cases |
|---|---|---|---|
| + | + (B-spline) | +d side | 338 |
| + | + (swept) | +d side | 51 |
| - | - (B-spline) | -d side | 395 |
| - | - (swept) | -d side | 13 |

These count curve/offset pairs, with the same offset counted once per curve it bounds. The
remaining 112 have a chart that's within 1e-6 m of neither side, which happens where a chart point
is past the basis's edge (below). None contradicts the sense. The offset's own sense has
equalled its basis's every time, so the sample can't say which flag Parasolid reads. The converter
applies the basis's sense, in the evaluator and in the STEP writer (STEP's OFFSET_SURFACE runs along
its basis's natural normal).

Next was blend 3443, whose spine (offset 3441 ∩ offset 3440, both 9 mm off B-splines) ran for over
17 minutes. Two things were slow:

- The foot on an offset was found by Gauss-Newton on the offset surface itself, with derivatives by
  differences, and a 1e-15 stopping test it never met, so it always ran all 60 steps. The foot on
  an offset has the same (u, v) as the foot on its basis, since both lie on one normal line, so it's
  the basis's foot now. The basis's own Newton was plain Gauss-Newton too, which converges only
  linearly 9 mm off the surface, so it gained the curvature term (second derivatives by differencing
  the exact first ones) and also stops once a step moves the point less than 1e-14 m.
- Chart points 15 to 18 of the spine lie past the lower v edge of B-spline 3438 (`edge.js`).
  Clamped to the edge, they're 30 µm to 1.26 mm further than 9 mm from it. Carrying the boundary
  span's polynomial on gets within 2 to 94 µm, still not exact. Carrying the surface on along its
  edge tangent, S(u, v0) + (v − v0)·Sv(u, v0), puts all four exactly 9 mm away (to well under a
  nanometre). So past its knot range a B-spline surface carries on flat, along its edge tangent.
  The evaluator does that now, and an offset's foot is allowed out past its basis's edge.

With those, blend 3443's spine fits to 9.8e-10 m (`spinefit.js`), but it still takes 264 s on the
power-saving profile.

## Regression check

The whole online sample was resolved before and after (`resolve-after.txt`, against EXP-085's
`resolve-after-march.txt`). 132 parts resolve, up from 130. 5 have no partition stream. No part
got a larger fit error. Two got much better:

| part | worst curve error before | after |
|---|---|---|
| `a4ecc6f5` | 499 m | 9.8e-9 m |
| `5f8e9db3` | 3.5e-2 m | 8.9e-9 m |

Both had curves through rolling-ball blends that were fitted against the fitted blend. The test
suite passes (61, 2 skipped).

`resolve-sweep.js` in sldprt-format-research/tools reruns this check against any baseline.

After those, head_back got through 88 of its blends in about 5 minutes, then stopped on blend 3141
(r = 0.2 mm). Its spine is plane 3140 ∩ blend 3139, and blend 3139 (r = 1.7 mm) has a closed spine:
an intersection of cone 3158 and offset 3157 whose start and end are the same `H` limit, with a
20-point chart that starts and ends exactly there (`b3141.js`). The converter found the chart point
nearest the start and the one nearest the end, got the same one, and fell back to a march that
stopped at once. The spine came out as a two-point line of zero length, so the pipe round it was
3.5 to 5.5 mm from blend 3141's chart. Following a closed chart all the way round fixes it, and all
four chart points of 3141's spine are then exactly 1.7 mm from 3139's spine.

With every blend fitted, head_back stopped on edge 362, a ring with no vertices: the contact of
blend 3162 with offset 3430 all the way round its closed spine. The contact curve is now fitted
round the whole spine and runs the way the edge's chart does. head_back then resolves, in about
20 minutes, but not within tolerance yet. The worst fitted curve is 4.7e-8 m and the worst fitted
surface 2.4e-7 m off the exact data, where every other part that resolves is within 1e-8 m. So it
doesn't count as passing yet, and those two are next.

## Second look: trying to break the two claims

Both claims went to a fresh agent that hadn't seen any of the above. Each was told to break its
claim with its own code, and not to trust the converter's uncommitted evaluator.

**Offset side** (its scripts: `debunk1/`, copied to `scripts/EXP086/debunk1/`). It survived.
Counted by distinct surface, 62 offsets bound a charted intersection (460 curve/surface pairs)
and 39 carry faces with 1,180 vertices. Every point sits on the side d × sense × (du × dv) gives,
and none on the flipped side. Nothing sits near zero distance, so a coincidence isn't plausible.
`true_offset` is false on every node and `scale` is null on those printed. The agent read the raw
nodes and agrees the parser reads `sense` right. The one thing it couldn't break apart is that in
all 592 OFFSET_SURF nodes of the sample, the offset's sense equals its basis's. So which flag
Parasolid reads is still open. Only 4 files have offsets at all (head_back, head_face, 0739c559
and 2f121983), and every basis is a B_SURFACE or SWEPT_SURF. The converter had a comment saying head_back has
144 offsets of blends. It has 144 offsets, but 137 are of B-splines and 7 of swept surfaces, so the
comment was wrong and is fixed. Its "offset of a blend" case never runs on the sample.

**Tangent extension** (`debunk2/`). It survived. With its own Cox-de Boor evaluator, chart points
15 to 18 of blend 3443's spine sit at these distances from 9 mm (µm):

| point | clamped | end span carried on | quadratic | tangent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 | +26.3 | −2.3 | −2.3 | 0.000 |
| 16 | +252.1 | −21.0 | −21.0 | 0.000 |
| 17 | +717.8 | −58.7 | −58.7 | 0.000 |
| 18 | +1148.8 | −94.0 | −93.0 | 0.000 |

Extending the offset surface along its own tangent, rather than the basis, is close (up to 3.2 µm)
but not exact. Over the whole sample, past-the-edge points turn up only in head_back (28 points,
all within 1 µm of the tangent model) and head_face (160 of the 162 that matter within 1 µm. The
other 2 fit at 0.04 µm or better once searched more densely, and those were past the edge in u,
not v). Corners, rational surfaces and extensions over about 2 parameter units aren't covered.
It also noticed vertices 0.1 to 1 mm off thread and degree-1 surfaces in 5 parts (d799e9a3,
073482d3, 0d68d32d, db8a97e2, f0b9a118), inside the knot range. That's unexplained and is a new
question.

## A regression caught on the way

The first version of the faster foot (the curvature term, and stopping once a step moves less than
1e-14 m) also applied to plain B-spline feet. On 5f8e9db3's spring it took the worst curve error
from 8.9e-9 m to 2.2e-3 m. Switching each change off on its own didn't restore it. Switching both
off did, so both let the foot settle on a neighbouring coil. They're now used only for the foot on
an offset, and the spring is back to 8.93e-9 m.
