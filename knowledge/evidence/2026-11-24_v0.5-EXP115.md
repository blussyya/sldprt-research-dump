# EXP-115: boundary cycles on seam faces (NQ-072)

2026-11-24. The display reader gave up on 300 faces ("Ambiguous boundary graph"). On the 159 in
modern files there are two causes: an edge used twice by the same face, which is a seam on a sphere,
cylinder or torus and a slit on a plane, and two loops touching at a vertex. Both now get their
cycles by walking the boundary through the triangle fans. On the 20,788 faces the old code
handled, the new walk gives the same cycles. The other 141 faces are all in chainwheel, a pre-2011
file, and stay unresolved.

Measured, not blind-checked. Converter commit babdec9. Scripts: `scripts/EXP115/` (`ambiguous.js`, `sectors.js`,
`check_sectors.js`, `perfile.js`), outputs next to them. Converter: `src/display.js` (`sectorCycles`),
`src/convert.js`, `test/display-cycles.test.js`.

## Why the graph was ambiguous

The old code welds vertices by exact coordinates, joins the boundary segments (nonzero Block1 id)
and requires every vertex to have two neighbours (`ambiguous.out`):

- `ef1d5cfe`, 24 faces (spheres): a seam edge whose two fins are both on the face meets the rim. 32
  vertices have 3 neighbours and 46 have 4. 24 of the edges at those vertices are seams in the
  native B-rep.
- `2f121983`, 9 faces (planes, a cylinder, a torus): the same, with an edge used twice by the face
  (on the planes, a slit drawn as one segment).
- `0132519a`, 1 face (a cylinder with two loops): four vertices with 4 neighbours and no seam. The
  two loops touch at those vertices.

## The fix

Every boundary segment is a cut. Each triangle that owns a cut segment gives one half-edge, so a
seam has one half-edge on each side. Around a vertex, triangles joined across uncut edges form a
sector, and a sector holds exactly two half-edge ends, which are consecutive along the loop. The
walk follows half-edges from sector to sector. A seam is walked once on each side, so a cycle can
pass the same vertices twice.

The reader keeps the old code for every face it handled before, and uses the walk only where the
old code gave up. If the walk fails too, the old error stays.

## Checks

| check | result |
|---|---|
| faces the old code handled (whole corpus, 222 files): same cycles, as sets of welded segments | 20,788 of 20,788 |
| modern faces it gave up on, now traced, each half-edge used once | 159 of 159, in 11 parts (`perfile.out`) |
| segments walked twice (seams and slits) | 268 |
| chainwheel (pre-2011) | 141 faces, a boundary chain ends at a vertex with no partner; unchanged |
| synthetic cylinder band with a seam (`test/display-cycles.test.js`) | one cycle of 10 half-edges: 4 + 4 ring, 2 seam |
| the same band without the seam id | two rings, as before |

Exports: mesh STEP (`--mesh`) builds exact planes from cycles. A cycle that passes a vertex twice
isn't a simple polygon, so those faces keep their triangles. Mesh STEP is unchanged on `2f121983`,
`ef1d5cfe` and `0132519a` (EXP-116 `meshstep.js`). Exact STEP doesn't use the display.
