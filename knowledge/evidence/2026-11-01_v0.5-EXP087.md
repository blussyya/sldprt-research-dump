# EXP-087: a denser fit check (in progress, not verified)

Converter code is on `solidworks-converters` branch `wip/dense-fit-check`, not `dev`.
Scripts and outputs are in `scripts/EXP087/`.

## Why

EXP-086's blind check found edge 826 on `82981f2a` (head_back) at 1.12e-8 m, 77% along the curve,
where the fitter's own check saw 9.7e-9. The fitter checks each interval at 3 points (0.25, 0.5,
0.75), and the error sat between them.

## What changed

`fitPoints` in `src/brep/fit.js`: once the 3-point check passes, a dense pass checks 8 more points
per interval (0.1 ... 0.9) on the same curve. If it finds a bad interval, that interval is split
and the 3-point rounds carry on until they pass, then another dense pass.

## Results so far

- Edge 826, by the blind check's own script (`EXP086/debunk4/diag826.js`, 16,123 samples):
  worst 9.44e-9 m, none over 1e-8. Before: 1.12e-8 m, 60 samples over. (`diag826-new.txt`)
- Online sweep (`sweep087b.txt`, code at 770ddca): nothing worse than the EXP-086 baseline apart
  from head_back. `f0b9a118` went from 1.6e-8 to 9.95e-9 m, `2e6e140a` from 2.7e-8 to 1.38e-8.
  `3c4752f1` is unchanged at 3.95e-8.
- `npm test`: 61 pass, 2 skipped (code at a0de468; not rerun since).

## Two problems the dense pass turned up

**1. polyFn depended on its last call.** The corrector in `polyFn` seeded its surface feet from
whatever it computed last. The first dense pass jumped from the end of a curve back to the start,
and on the thread parts (`db8a97e2`, `d799e9a3`, `0d68d32d`) the feet landed a turn away: points
3e-3 m off, every interval flagged, and the parts timed out (300 s, against 17 to 38 s before).
Fixed in daacbc2: each polyline vertex's feet are found once, in order, each seeded from the one
before, and every call seeds from its own segment's start vertex. `db8a97e2` now takes 28 s.

**2. head_back gets worse.** With daacbc2, head_back takes 77 min (about 20 before) and blend 3369's
spine (face 2478) fits to 8e-2 m. On `dev` it fits to 4.4e-10 m in 18 s. With the dense pass
switched off, daacbc2 also gets 4.4e-10 m, so the cause is the dense pass, not the seeding.
(`hberr-daacbc2.txt`)

`spine3369.png` shows what happens. The fit is within about 1e-11 m of the exact curve everywhere.
A few single exact points, among good neighbours, are 3e-5 to 5e-5 m off it. The dense pass flags
them, the fitter splits those intervals to chase them, and after 24 rounds the fit is ruined.

What those points are, so far: every corrector result checked lies on offset surface 3432 to
about 1e-17 m by its seeded foot. But an unseeded foot search from the same point finds another
spot on 3432, 2 to 3 cm away, only 1e-5 to 2e-5 m from it. So 3432 seems to come back within tens
of microns of itself, and at some dense samples the corrector settles on that other sheet.

**Correction (same day): that reading was wrong.** Two blind agents were given the claim: one to
prove it, one to break it. Both rejected it, each with its own scripts (`scripts/EXP087/prove/`
and `disprove/`). The surface was also misnamed. It's offset 3368, the spine's own surface
(`offset3368 x plane3367`), not 3432.

What both found:

- There's no second sheet. Nothing far apart in parameter on 3368 comes near the spine: no closer
  than 1.07 cm (prove, 0.75-unit grid) or 2 mm (disprove, 1 to 2 mm grid). Neither grid could
  rule out a self-approach somewhere else on the surface.
- The bad points are real and come back reliably. They're 3.4e-5 to 4.6e-5 m off the curve, in
  the last 7 to 9 mm near one end.
- They're not on 3368. Measured independently (basis plus offset along the normal), a bad point
  is 3.3e-5 to 4.4e-5 m off the surface, while a good neighbour is 1e-12 m off.
- That stretch of the spine lies on the B-spline's flat extension past its knot range: v about 71
  to 77, with the knot range ending at v = 47.5587, 24 to 30 units (about 3.7 cm) past the edge.
- There, the foot finder sometimes gives back a foot clamped to the knot edge, at v = 47.5587,
  3.7 to 4.5 cm from the point. Its distance comes out near 0 only because the point sits close
  to the edge foot's tangent plane.
- The disprove agent traced the corrector step by step on a bad point. The foot flips between the
  true foot and the clamped one. Newton steps of about 3.4e-5 m go back and forth, and after 40
  iterations the loop just stops and returns the last point. It never converged, and the corrector
  doesn't check that it did (only its damped path checks).

Not pinned down: why the surface inverse falls back to the clamped foot. The prove agent says its
free Newton converges slowly out in the extension and gives up. That fits but wasn't traced
inside `surfaceInverse`.

## Is the extension real, or our misreading? (checked against SolidWorks 2026's x_t)

Kevin R exported head_back from SolidWorks 2026 as x_t (Parasolid 37.1) and STEP
(`test files online/2013/exports-SW2026/`). In that x_t, written by Parasolid itself:

- A surface node of type 125 stores its parameter range as u -88.4790184368548 to
  66.0166594551734, v -45.7323647405839 to 47.5586737582817. That's exactly the range we read for
  B-spline 3436, the basis of offset 3368. So the knot range does end at v = 47.5587; we weren't
  misreading it.
- The spine's end point, -0.0481014350995936 .0070817627028915 -.0458425378176708, is stored in
  the x_t too, next to its start point .0209389511887813 .008415... By our reading that end is at
  v = 77.72 on the same surface, 30 units past its range.

So Parasolid itself keeps a fillet's spine running past the end of the B-spline the spine lies on,
and treats the surface as carrying on there. The extension is Parasolid's, not ours. Still open:
whether its extension rule is exactly ours (flat along the edge tangent). Our chart points sit on
our extension to 1.4e-15 m, which says yes, but that's measured with our own evaluator.

## Display mesh against SolidWorks' STEP (2026-11-02)

Kevin also re-saved head_back as SLDPRT from SolidWorks 2026
(`exports-SW2026/82981f2a_head_back-2026.sldprt`). Both display meshes were measured against his
SW2026 STEP with OpenCascade, by distance from every mesh vertex and triangle centre to the nearest
STEP face (`scripts/EXP087/stlstep/`, `measure.py`, `summary*.txt`, `dist*.csv`). Units are mm.

| mesh | vertices: max / median | centres: max | points over 0.1 mm |
|---|---|---|---|
| original (SW2013 display list) | 0.288 / 1.6e-6 | 0.305 | 271 |
| SW2026 re-save | 0.0011 / 1.4e-6 | 0.086 | 0 |

- Apart from one strip, both meshes lie on SolidWorks' B-rep, and the triangle centres differ only
  by normal chord sag.
- In the original, every point over 0.1 mm is in one strip at x -28 to 28, y 30 to 49, z -66 to
  -70. It's nearest STEP faces 171, 173, 459 and 186: plane 173 and three small cylinders.
- Near plane 173 all 70 points are 0.200 to 0.205 mm off it, on the same side. That's a flat sheet
  shifted off the plane, not sag.
- Both B-reps (2013 and 2026) hold that plane (offset 0.0000 from the STEP) and another plane
  parallel to it, 0.1975 mm below (nodes 10156 and 11621; `plane173.js`). The old mesh sits on the
  lower one.
- The SW2026 mesh has none of this. Its points near 173 are all on the STEP's plane.

So the old file's saved display list was out of date with its own B-rep in that strip, and SW2026's
rebuild regenerated it. It isn't a misreading on our part: the same reader gives the right mesh for
the 2026 file.

### Why the old mesh is out of date (follow-up, same day)

- **The file says so itself.** `ModelStamps` holds (1463079739, 119, 117), and the first value read as
  Unix time is 2016-05-12. `DLUpdateStamp` holds 107. The display list was last brought up to date at
  model stamp 107; the model is at 119. In the SW2026 re-save, `ModelStamps` is unchanged and
  `DLUpdateStamp` is 119. (`stamps.js`)
- **What changed between 107 and 119** is the frame around the three rectangular openings on the
  back (`deviation_from_below.png`). Old display faces 23, 24, 26 and 31 sit 0.10 mm higher than in
  the 2026 mesh, at the same x/y, with about 11% more area. Old face 234 (the big plane, 393 mm2)
  is 315 mm2 in the 2026 mesh, and four 2026 faces (115, 235, 239, 240; about 92 mm2) have no old
  counterpart. So the design was edited there by tenths of a mm and some faces were split off.
  (`facecmp.js`; display faces matched by area and centroid)
- **The lower plane is used.** Faces 6564 and 8890 lie on plane 10156 in the old B-rep, and faces
  11611 and 6749 on plane 11621 in the 2026 one. (`lowplane.js`)
- **Nothing in the file holds the old geometry apart from the mesh.** Every body section
  (Partition and ResolvedFeatures, in both files) has the STEP's planes. The `(deltas)` section
  (79 KB) doesn't parse with our reader. A raw scan of it finds no plane parallel to face 173, while
  the same scan run on the body finds both known planes. (`allsections.js`, `deltascan.js`)
- **Face ids weren't scrubbed.** Every face in the old display list carries a metadata block
  (`legacyTail`) that our reader doesn't decode yet. The 2026 file's metadata stops with
  "Unexpected array header" in our reader. So neither file gives a face-id mapping today; that's our
  reader's gap, not the file's. The 535 = 535 count match in the 2026 file is a count match only.
- **The old file wasn't otherwise broken.** Its B-rep passes all 34,249 graph checks and matches the
  STEP. The 532 against 535 face count is the stale mesh missing the faces split off after stamp 107.
- **How common a stamp gap is** (`stampsurvey.js`, 142 files): 109 match, 24 have a display stamp
  behind the model stamp, and 9 have no stamps. Of the 24, 11 rows didn't parse in the face-count
  test, and of the other 13 only head_back has a different count. The same project's head_face and
  camera_support have the same 119/107 gap but matching counts; a count can't see shifted
  geometry, though. A gap means the mesh may be stale, not that it's wrong.
- **The save history** (`Biography`, not decoded; strings only) lists several SW2013 SP1 sessions,
  starting from the stock Part template and then in two project folders. Nothing in it shows which
  session left the mesh stale.

Not checked yet: the 24 stamp-gap files by geometry (the OCC check takes about 30 min a file);
whether this frame is what Kevin's overlay showed (`deviation_from_below.png` is there to compare);
the deltas format; and how SolidWorks came to save without updating the display list. A save after
a rebuild with no graphics pass, such as a batch or API save, would fit, but there's no evidence for
it.


## Comparing Kevin's screenshots (2026-11-04, draft for review)

Kevin's screenshots show blue patches around the three openings, but also across the shell,
its rim and its internal supports. The stale mesh explains the measured shift around the
openings. We cannot tell from the screenshots whether it explains any particular blue patch.

A fresh agent independently checked the archived distance CSVs. All 271 old-mesh samples more
than 0.1 mm from STEP are inside x -27.6393 to 27.7619 mm, y 29.7248 to 48.8232 mm and
z -69.8209 to -66.3456 mm. The 2026 mesh has no recorded sample over 0.1 mm. Outside the cyan
box, the largest recorded distances are 0.081897 mm for the old mesh and 0.086288 mm for the
2026 mesh. These are checks of the existing measurements, not a new OpenCascade run.

The agent rejected the claim that all the blue patches mark the stale frame. Some patches
are near the frame, but matching individual patches needs the original overlay setup or
geometry. Vertex and triangle-centre samples do not bound the error everywhere on a triangle.

One earlier claim above also needs narrowing: the raw scan found no matching plane in the
undecoded deltas. That does not prove that no encoding of the earlier geometry exists there.
The mesh is the only record of the earlier shape identified so far.
