# EXP-113: are the off-surface display vertices chord points or edge-curve points?

2026-11-24. Both kinds exist. The bulk of the display vertices that sit off a cylinder or cone face
are chord points: they lie on the straight chord between their boundary neighbours, and they are as
far from the face's edge curve as from the surface. The off-chord exceptions that EXP-105's blind
checks listed are edge-curve points: they lie on a tolerant B-spline edge's curve, and that curve
sits off the face's surface by less than the edge's tolerance. The question came from the user
("couldn't they just be NURBS points?"), and it is right for the exceptions.

Measured, not blind-checked. Script: `scripts/EXP113/curvepoints.js`; outputs `run1.out` to
`run3.out` and one `.jsonl` per part, one row per off vertex and edge.

## Method

For each display face joined to a native cylinder or cone (`src/brep/display-join.js`, converter
`main` 881bf75), every vertex further than 1e-7 m from the surface (above float32 rounding on these
parts, EXP-105) is measured three ways:

- `d_surf`, its distance to the face's own surface;
- `d_curve`, its distance to the curve of each Block1 edge it sits on, found by the edge's display
  id among the face's native edges. Intersection and blend curves are taken after the procedural
  resolve, so they are the fitted B-splines (within 1e-8 m of exact);
- `d_chord`, its distance to the chord between its two neighbours on the same edge.

"On the curve" means `d_curve` under the larger of 1e-7 m and a tenth of `d_surf`.

Control: on-surface boundary vertices (`d_surf` under 1e-9 m) have to sit on their edge curve. Up to
400 per part: largest `d_curve` 4.2e-10 m (C21), 2.2e-10 (`8cd79ff5`), 4.7e-9 (`d0d61cde`), 1.1e-10
(BME280), 1.9e-8 (Dekor), 2.0e-9 (`a4ecc6f5`). On `2f121983`, which doesn't resolve, it is 3.4e-7 m,
because its trimmed curves belong to tolerant edges and aren't fitted.

On circle and ellipse edges no off vertex is on the curve (0 of 2,186). A circle edge of a cylinder
lies on the cylinder, so a point on it would be on the surface. These vertices sit inside the surface
(medians 1.7e-7 to 1.9e-5 m by part), on the straight chord, which is where a neighbouring face's tessellation point
lands when it is inserted into this face's boundary polyline.

## Edge-curve points: the off-chord exceptions

| part | off-chord vertices | `d_curve` | `d_surf` | edge tolerance |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| `d0d61cde` | 14 | 3.2e-11 to 1.4e-9 m | 1.0e-7 to 9.9e-7 m | 5.0e-7 to 1.8e-6 m |
| Dekor | 2 | 2.2e-8 and 3.2e-8 m | 1.2e-7 and 1.5e-7 m | 5.0e-7 m |
| `2f121983` | 6 | 1.2e-7 to 2.6e-7 m | the same | 4.0e-6 and 1.0e-5 m |

All are on tolerant B-spline or trimmed edges, 3e-6 to 4e-5 m from the chord. In `d0d61cde` all 14
sit on the fitted edge curve to 1.4e-9 m, so the vertex is the edge curve's point, and the curve
itself runs 1e-7 to 1e-6 m off this face's surface, inside the edge's tolerance. Dekor's two are on
the curve to 3e-8 m. In `2f121983` the curves aren't fitted (the part doesn't resolve), and the six
are 1.2e-7 to 2.6e-7 m from both the curve and the surface, also inside the tolerance.

So a tolerant edge's curve doesn't have to lie on both of its faces, and the mesh follows the curve.
That is what Parasolid's tolerant edges allow.
