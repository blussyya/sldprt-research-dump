## Question

EXP-075 established that the body decoded from `Config-0-Partition` equals SolidWorks' STEP export
of the same part. Can we write that body out as STEP ourselves, exactly (analytic surfaces and
B-splines, no triangles), so that the file is the same solid as SolidWorks' export?

A first writer that mapped every native face, loop and edge one to one round-tripped **42 of 49**
controlled models. This note records why the other 7 failed, the fixes, and the checks that the
fixed writer's output is the same solid as SolidWorks'.

## Why 7 models failed

Two places where Parasolid's topology has no direct STEP equivalent, both found by reading the
failures back against the native body with EXP-075's comparison:

| models | failure | cause |
|---|---|---|
| C13_cone ×2, C25_cone_sw2011_native | apex vertex missing: nearest STEP vertex is 11.2 mm away | The cone's apex is a loop with one fin and no edge. `native.js` skipped fins without an edge, so the loop came out empty and the writer dropped it. |
| C14_sphere ×2, C15_torus ×2 | the sphere / torus face has no STEP face | A whole sphere or torus is one Parasolid face with **zero loops**. STEP's `ADVANCED_FACE` needs at least one bound. |

The fixes:

- **Vertex loops.** `native.js` now keeps a loop whose fins have no edge as `{coedges:[], vertex}`;
  `step/write.js` writes it as `VERTEX_LOOP`, and `step/read.js` reads `VERTEX_LOOP` back. SolidWorks'
  export takes the other route: it splits the cone into two halves whose seam edges run up to the
  apex, so the apex is an ordinary vertex. Both are valid AP214; the vertex loop keeps the face one
  to one with Parasolid's.
- **Splitting boundary-less faces** (`brep/seams.js`). A whole sphere becomes two halves along the
  meridians at u = 0 and u = π, pole to pole. A whole torus becomes four quarters along two meridians
  and two parallels. New seam edges are trimmed circles. Each piece keeps the face's surface and
  outward side; loop direction is reversed when the face's sense is opposite to the surface's.
  Any other closed surface with no boundary throws; none occurs in the corpus.
- **Ring edges** (no change in outcome, recorded for completeness). A closed edge with no vertex,
  such as the circle round a through hole, gets one vertex at the start of its curve's range, used
  as both ends. Two-loop faces on closed surfaces (a hole's cylinder) are written as Parasolid has
  them, with no seam; OpenCascade accepts them as valid (below).

## Result: controlled models

| | naive writer | fixed writer |
|---|---|---|
| reads back as the native body (EXP-075 comparison, 1e-8 m) | 42/49 | **49/49** |
| OpenCascade: valid, exactly one solid | 45/49 (the 4 sphere/torus files are open shells) | **49/49** |
| OpenCascade boolean difference with SolidWorks' `model.step`, both directions | | **0 on 49/49** |

The cone files are valid for OpenCascade even without the apex loop, since the lateral face still
closes; they fail the round trip because the body has lost a vertex.

### Exact volume

`lib/brep/volume.js` integrates the enclosed volume from the surfaces and curves, with no
tessellation: the divergence theorem turns it into a sum over faces of ∫∫ (p · n) dA / 3, and
Green's theorem in each face's (u, v) parameters turns each face term into a boundary integral.
Planes use the shoelace area. For periodic parameters the boundary integral is taken from a
reference v (the apex of a cone, the contained pole of a sphere) or with a branch cut placed in
the widest angular gap, its side chosen by the loop's orientation. B-spline faces split the inner
integral at knots. Faces with no boundary are split as above first.

| comparison | result |
|---|---|
| our STEP vs SolidWorks' STEP, same integrator | worst relative difference **5.6e-15** over 49 models |
| our STEP vs closed form (12 models with a build dimension per era) | worst relative difference **4.3e-15** over 24 files |
| native body vs our STEP | identical on all 49 |

Closed forms used: cubes, shell (424 mm³), chamfer (995 mm³), holes, cone πr²h/3, sphere 4πr³/3,
torus 2π²Rr², quarter torus, half cylinder, and C20's crossed cylinders 2πr²L − 16r³/3.

### OpenCascade's volumes, and C20

OpenCascade's `BRepGProp` volume equals ours to 1e-12 relative on 47/49. The two that differ are
both copies of C20_cylinders_crossed (identical bodies in both eras):

| C20 volume, mm³ | value | − closed form |
|---|---|---|
| closed form, 1000π − 2000/3 | 2474.925986923 | |
| ours, exact integrator, on our file or SolidWorks' | 2474.925986923 | 3e-12 |
| OpenCascade on our file | 2474.926120807 | +1.3e-4 |
| OpenCascade on SolidWorks' file | 2474.926039661 | +5.3e-5 |
| OpenCascade on the boolean common of the two | 2474.926133257 | +1.5e-4 |

The boolean difference is zero both ways, so the two files are the same solid. OpenCascade gives
three different volumes for three decompositions of that one solid, and all three miss the closed
form, while our integrator matches it to 3e-12. The error is in OpenCascade's numerical
integration of this solid, most likely on the cylinder faces bounded by the ellipses where the
cylinders meet, and not in either file.

### C16, the loft

C16_loft_spline has one B-spline face whose boundary circles are Parasolid tolerant edges: declared
tolerance 2.07e-6 m, and the circle lies 1.9e-8 m off the cubic surface. The volume of such a solid
is only defined to the width of that gap:

| C16, mm³ | SW2011 | SW2022 |
|---|---|---|
| SolidWorks measured (BUILD_LOG) | 893.824910 | 739.841364 |
| ours, on our file and on SolidWorks' file | 893.833597 | 739.831480 |
| OpenCascade, on either file | 893.827180 | 739.818761 |

Ours equals SolidWorks' own STEP to 1e-13 because the two files have identical control nets and
knots. Integrating Green's form along the edge curves and integrating the surface directly over the
same region differ by about 3e-3 mm³, consistent with that gap. SolidWorks, OpenCascade and our
integrator land within 0.025 mm³ (3e-5 relative) of each other. Nothing here points to a wrong
file; the loft's volume just is not defined more tightly than that.

## Result: production parts

`production.js` tries every part in `test files original/` (24 files, including the 13 copies of
the controlled cubes in `controlled/`):

| outcome | parts |
|---|---|
| exact STEP, reads back as the body | 15: PTC GE8080-8, cube.SLDPRT, and the 13 `controlled/` copies |
| blocked: `INTERSECTION` curve | distributor main boss, Helical Bevel Gear, Pocket Wheel, USB hub case BOTTOM |
| blocked: edge with no curve (curve held on its fins) | Dekor, USB hub case TOP |
| no `Config-0-Partition` (pre-2011) | SW2000-s01, chainwheel, plate4 |

Counting only the independent real parts with a body, **2 of 8 are exact** (PTC: 126 faces,
81662.185970 mm³, equal to OpenCascade's volume of the same file). The other 6 are each blocked by
one of the two NQ-049 types; the package falls back to the display mesh for them.

## What this establishes

- A STEP file written from the decoded body is the same solid as SolidWorks' own export on every
  controlled model: same topology at 1e-8 m, boolean difference zero, volumes equal to 6e-15.
- The 7 initial failures were two missing translation rules (vertex loops, boundary-less closed
  faces), not decoding errors.
- Exact volume is now a measurement we can make from the file alone.

Promoted as INV-028.

## Limits

- No controlled model contains `INTERSECTION` curves, curveless edges, `SWEPT_SURF` or
  `BLENDED_EDGE`; they block 6 of 8 real parts (NQ-049).
- Unit is millimetres, AP214, one body per file. Colours, names and assemblies are not written.
- The writer has no tolerant-edge handling: it writes the curves as stored, which is what
  SolidWorks' export also does on C16.
