# EXP-085 — Exactness, the parts with no display data, and the first community files

**Question.** EXP-084 left three things open: whether the 17 parts with no display mesh really
have none, why some fitted geometry and STL volumes disagreed, and the B-rep triangulation failing
on 56 of 136 parts. New files also came in from the community: five re-saves of parts
already in the dump (four saved in 2026, one then saved as 2025), a 2022 part and a 2026 part.

**Answer.** The 17 parts have no tessellation anywhere in the file. The disagreements came from
bugs in our reading, fitting and volume integral, plus two places where the reference itself is
off: SolidWorks' STEP of the loft control doesn't close, and its STEP of the crossed cylinders has
B-splines where the file has ellipses. Our exact STEP matches SolidWorks' own STEP to about 1e-16
in volume on all 49 controls. The bugs found here were making the fitted geometry on some online
parts wrong by up to kilometres without saying so, and exact export now refuses rather than write
that. Details and numbers below; scripts in `scripts/EXP085/`.

## 1. The DisplayLists version is the release that last wrote the stream

EXP-084 noted files whose DisplayLists version is older than the release that saved them (DL 3800
in a 2024 save). Across all 175 modern files with a History, the DisplayLists version is always one
of the releases in that file's own History. In 157 it's the last save. In the other 18 it's an
earlier one: the stream was written by that release and carried through later saves unchanged
(`dlhistory.js`).

`4febfc67` (DL 3800, saved 2024) went 95+ → 98 → 2000 → 2001 → 2003 → 2007 → 2008 → 2009 → 2015 →
2024; its stream is from the 3800 release. A re-save doesn't always do this: the four community
re-exports (section 3) all got a fresh DL 19000 when saved in 2026. What decides whether a save
rewrites the stream isn't known. One guess is opening an old part without rebuilding it. Saving
as an older release rewrites it too: plate4 saved as 2025 from 2026 (section 3) has DL 18000.

Four of the five stub DisplayLists from EXP-084 are carried-over streams (`b32017f5` DL 10000 in a
2020 save; `2693f32a`, `c292e7e2`, `d889c6fc` DL 16000 in 2026 saves).

## 2. Parts saved without display data have no mesh anywhere

For the 17 parts, every stream was searched for the tessellation class names and for runs of
float32 unit vectors, which any mesh has thousands of (`meshhunt.js`). No stream has either the
class names or more than 160 unit-vector hits.

The hits that do show up were traced to what owns them (`unitregions.js`, `unitvec.js`,
`rotations.js`). Most "float32 unit vectors" aren't vectors: they're a single ±1.0f between small
integers, and the denormals around it (1.4e-43 and so on) are integers read as floats. With those
removed, no class has more than 14. The float64 triples are:

- `moView_c`: rotation matrices of the standard views. Back (−1,0,0 | 0,1,0 | 0,0,−1), Left,
  Right, Top, Bottom, then Isometric (0.7071, −0.4082, 0.5774 …), Trimetric and Dimetric. Front,
  the identity, isn't among them.
- `moDirectionLight_c`, `moAmbientLight_c`: light directions.
- `moVisualProperties_c`, `moFeatColorTab_c`: colours that happen to have unit length.
- The span from `moRelMgr_c` to `moEnvFolder_c`, which holds the feature data, plus
  `moAnnotationView_c` and `moCThreadRefMgr_c`.

None of it is geometry of the part. Several of these parts look converter-made: names ending
`.stp.SLDPRT` or `body1`, 12 or 13 streams instead of 30–40, and `4df87793` has a lowercase
`config-0` stream.

## 4. Drawing a line with the controls

Every controlled model has SolidWorks' own STEP and STL. For all 49, each way of getting a volume
was compared with OpenCascade's volume of SolidWorks' STEP (`ctlvol.js`, `controls-volumes.json`,
`controls-ocp.jsonl`):

| route | against SolidWorks' STEP |
|---|---|
| our exact STEP, through OpenCascade | 48 within 3e-16, C20 (crossed cylinders) 2.3e-8 |
| our volume integrator on the B-rep | within 4e-14, except the loft (8e-6 in 2011, 1.7e-5 in 2022) and C20 (2e-8); see section 5b |
| our triangulation at 1e-5 m | planes exact; curved parts 5e-5 to 3e-3 low or high |
| our triangulation at 1e-6 m | curved parts 3e-6 to 3e-4 |
| SolidWorks' 2022 STL | 3e-5 to 4e-3 |
| SolidWorks' 2011 STL | up to 61% low (see below) |
| the display mesh | 7e-8 to 5e-3 |

So the line is: **exact STEP is exact**, our STEP and SolidWorks' agree to the last digits.
**A triangle mesh can't be exact on a curved face.** What it can promise is that every vertex
lies on the exact surface and no triangle is further than the tolerance from it. At 1e-5 m our
mesh is as close as SolidWorks' own STL, and at 1e-6 m it's about ten times closer.

The two exceptions in the table turned out to be the reference, not us (section 5b). C20 has a
closed form, and our integral matches it to 1.6e-15 where SolidWorks' STEP is 2.1e-8 off. The
loft's file doesn't close, so OpenCascade's volume of either STEP depends on where it centres
the integral; ours matches OpenCascade on a watertight rebuild to 2e-9.

SolidWorks 2011's STL files in the corpus are open meshes with whole faces missing. C04's has 44
bottom triangles and no top face, 44 open edges. C10 has 8 open edges and C21 188. The sphere's
is closed. The low volumes come from the missing faces, not from 2011's tessellation, so these
files can't be used as a reference. The 2022 STLs are closed.

## 5. Bugs that made fitted geometry silently wrong

A sweep that only resolves the B-rep (`resolve.js`), before and after these fixes, is in
`resolve-before.txt` and `resolve-after-march.txt`. Before, several parts "resolved" with curve
errors of 0.5 mm to 24 km (`d799e9a3` 2.45e4 m, `a4ecc6f5` 499 m, `5f8e9db3` 126 mm, `c292e7e2`
4.1 mm, `3c4752f1` 3.1 mm, `0d68d32d` 0.53 mm), and exact STEP wrote them with that error as the
file's uncertainty. All of these are now within 10 nm. The causes:

- **node_ids are unique per body, not per partition.** `d0d61cde`'s solid and its four reference
  sheets each have an edge 7; the model keyed edges by node_id, so the solid's edge 7 took a
  sheet's line. That was every "vertices N mm off its curve" error in EXP-084: `d0d61cde`,
  `b32017f5`, `0d68d32d`, `4803f393`, `51f01927`.
- **SPUN_SURF has u = profile parameter, v = angle.** Our evaluator has them the other way, so
  SP_CURVEs on spun surfaces landed 2 m to 499 m from their vertices (`a4ecc6f5`). Read swapped,
  every end is within 1e-6 mm of its vertex (`spcurveconv.js`).
- **CONE measures v along the axis**, radius r + v·tan α. Ours measures along the slant. On
  `a4ecc6f5`'s 79° cone the SP_CURVEs were 35 mm off; read as axial, 1e-7 mm. STEP's conical
  surface is also axial; no PCURVE is written for cones or spun surfaces, so exported files were
  never affected.
- **The intersection march's stop test** stopped once B was less than a step ahead along the
  tangent, however far away. On `14f9214b` the end is 17 mm away across a loop but 2 mm ahead,
  so the curve became a straight chord, 15.6 mm off. The exact volume of that part changed from
  2,044,640 to 2,789,260 mm³ once it was fixed.
- **Projection onto B-surfaces** clamped u and v on surfaces closed in that direction and
  started Newton from the single nearest grid point. On `5f8e9db3`'s spring the nearest grid
  point can be on the next coil, 0.2 mm away. It now wraps and tries 8 starts.

Intersection curves now follow the chart Parasolid stores (falling back to marching where the
chart is too coarse). A constant-radius rolling-ball blend has an exact foot, which is how blends
whose spine meets another blend now resolve, since Parasolid uses a blend as the offset of a blend.
Exact STEP now throws if the fit is more than 1 µm off.

## 5b. The volume integral, and references that aren't exact

Run on our exact STEP files, OpenCascade disagreed with our volume integral on four online parts:
`4df87793` 16.3655 mm³ against our 0, `3980555c` 1817.016 against 4913.96, `0eab6495` 2700.360
against 2158.58, `065f38b0` 585890 against a negative number. Our triangulations sided with
OpenCascade. The integral was being run on the native faces, before the seam split the STEP
writer and the triangulator both use. Its H-form, which integrates from a reference v round loops
that wrap in u, goes wrong on faces whose loops don't simply wrap. `4df87793`'s sphere face has
three vertex-less circles as holes; `3980555c` has a cylinder face with a slit edge used in both
directions. Integrated after the split, all four agree with OpenCascade, the first three to 1e-10
or better. `volume()` now always splits a resolved model first, and refuses rather than
integrate unsplit if the split fails.

That left the loft (C16) at 1.7e-5 from OpenCascade on SolidWorks' STEP, and `065f38b0` at
2.6e-6. More Gauss points didn't move either, so it wasn't quadrature. Both have edges that
Parasolid gives a tolerance (C16's are 2.07 µm), and those edges don't lie exactly on their
faces. C16's top edges are exact circles of 4 mm radius; the side patches end on cubics that sit
up to 0.83 µm inside them (zero at the knots, `volcheck.js`, `loftclose.py`). SolidWorks' STEP has
the same gap, which OpenCascade reports as an edge deviation of 0.55 µm (2011) and 0.83 µm (2022).

A divergence-theorem volume is only independent of the origin on a closed shell. With the strip
uncovered, moving our integral's origin along the loft's axis moved the volume by 0.0028 mm³ per
mm. OpenCascade looks steady when you move the solid, but that's because it centres the integral
on the solid itself; the reference point you pass is ignored. Its answer is one point in the
same spread. For `065f38b0`, moving the origin from (0,0,0) to (440,0,0) mm changed the unclosed
volume by 0.94 mm³.

Two changes close it:

- For each coedge on a toleranced edge, the flux through the ribbon from the face's foot curve
  to the edge curve is added: ΔV = −(1/3)∫ R·(C′ × (C − F)) dt with R = (C + F)/2. That takes
  the drift from 0.0028 to 4e-5 mm³ per mm.
- On a B-spline face, the rate at which the foot moves along an edge that is off the surface
  also depends on the surface's curvature (the r·S_uu terms of the projection). Leaving them out
  cost 1e-4 mm³ per side face at a 0.83 µm gap. With them in, the drift is 2e-7 mm³ per mm.

As an independent check, `loftclose.py` rebuilds the loft in OpenCascade so it closes by
construction: the four full side patches, the original bottom, and a top face bounded by the
patches' own top rows instead of the circles. It sews with no free edges and is a valid solid.

| | 2011 loft | 2022 loft |
|---|---|---|
| OpenCascade, SolidWorks' STEP (doesn't close) | 893.827180 | 739.818761 |
| our integral before | 893.833597 | 739.831480 |
| our integral now | 893.819774 | 739.806315 |
| OpenCascade, watertight rebuild | 893.819774 | 739.806316 |

`065f38b0` is now 585892.368 mm³, moving by 4e-4 mm³ across origins 440 mm apart (7e-10).

C20, the crossed cylinders (r = 5 mm, 20 mm long, at right angles), has a closed form:
2πr²L − 16r³/3 = 2474.925986923 mm³. Our integral gives 2474.925986923 (1.6e-15). OpenCascade on
our STEP gives 2474.925983 (1.5e-9, its own quadrature), on SolidWorks' STEP 2474.926040 (2.1e-8).
The file stores the four intersection curves as ellipses, and our STEP writes them as ellipses.
SolidWorks' STEP writes them as B-splines.

The converter's `test/volume.test.js` checks both: C20 against the closed form, both lofts
against the rebuild, and that the loft's volume doesn't move with the origin.

## 6. Triangulation fixes from the controls

All 49 controls now close at 1e-5 and 1e-6 m (`openedges.js`). Three things opened them:

- A sphere split along u = 0 and u = π has two opposite seams as its whole boundary, and their
  circular mean can't say which half a face is. Both halves landed on [0, π]. The split now
  records each piece's cell.
- Bisecting an edge along a straight stretch of boundary (a cone's seam) put a point on the
  boundary that the next face didn't have.
- A diagonal flip could rebuild an edge through a point sitting on it to within rounding (the
  middle of a cylinder ruling), leaving a zero-area triangle.

## 7. The online sample after all this

All 141 files in the online list were triangulated at 1e-5 m, before and after this
experiment's fixes, with two minutes per part (`tess-before.txt`, `tess-after.txt`).

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| closed (no open or repeated edges) | 80 | 90 |
| open or repeated edges | 33 | 32 |
| error | 20 | 10 |
| over two minutes | 8 | 9 |

Before, 5 errors were fits that put vertices 2.8 cm to 21 cm off their curves (the node_id
collision), 5 were projections onto blends, and 3 were split chains. None of those remain. The
10 errors left:

- 5 files aren't SolidWorks parts. They sit in `test files online/not-a-part/` from EXP-083 and
  shouldn't have been in the sweep's list. Four are STEP files renamed `.SLDPRT` (`10582557`,
  `34a1cacb`, `9e494ca4`, `d7b58324`); `8ac95686` is base64 text that doesn't decode to any
  container we know. Refusing them is right.
- `2f121983`, `d2515121`: the intersection march doesn't reach the end of the edge.
- `3c4752f1`: two surfaces are tangent along the edge and the corrector finds no common point
  within 3.1 µm.
- `0739c559`: a blend's support face isn't found.
- `82981f2a`: interpolation band.

The 9 over two minutes include `5f8e9db3` and `a4ecc6f5`, which now resolve to within 10 nm but
take long to fit.

Of the 32 with gaps, 10 have only repeated edges (an edge used twice by the same face's
triangles) and no open ones. 11 have fewer than 30 open edges. The worst are `03764871` (2804
open), `d8206ed0` (2622), `ceefa2a9` (2186) and `8cd79ff5` (1058). `4df87793`, which had 514
repeated edges, is now closed. These are the triangulator's to fix, not the reading's: in every
one, the B-rep resolves.
