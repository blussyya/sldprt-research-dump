# EXP-075 — The native body against SolidWorks' STEP export

Captured 2026-10-03 UTC. Base: staging `94478c8`.

Run `node knowledge/evidence/scripts/EXP075/brep-vs-step.js --write` from the repository root.
[Script](scripts/EXP075/brep-vs-step.js), [module snapshot](scripts/EXP075/lib/README.md) and
[raw results with input hashes](scripts/EXP075/RESULTS.json). The same comparison runs as
`test/brep-step.test.js` in sldprt-format-research.

## Question

EXP-071–074 decoded the Parasolid body in `Config-0-Partition` and checked it for internal
consistency (graph checks) and against the display mesh (EXP-074). Neither establishes that the
body *is the part*. Every controlled model ships `model.step`, written by SolidWorks itself from the
same part. Does the decoded body equal it?

## Method

Both sides are reduced to the same neutral model: vertices, edges with curve and direction, faces
with surface, outward side and loops of oriented edges.

- **Native side:** `lib/brep/native.js` reads the typed nodes. Two conventions were measured here
  rather than assumed. `FIN.vertex` is the vertex at the **end** of the fin: on all 432 line edges,
  the other fin's vertex minus this one points against the line direction exactly when the
  curve's sense is `+`, and all 24 trimmed curves end at `FIN.vertex`. B-spline control points are
  stored with v varying fastest.
- **STEP side:** `lib/step/read.js` is a Part 21 parser plus an AP214 B-rep extractor, in
  millimetres converted to metres.

A STEP writer may split closed faces at seams, so the test (`lib/brep/compare.js`) is
correspondence modulo subdivision:

| check | requirement |
|---|---|
| vertices | every native vertex coincides with one STEP vertex |
| edges | every STEP edge lies on exactly one native edge (on its curve, inside its parameter extent), or is a seam |
| seams | a seam's two faces map to one native face, and that face's surface is closed (cylinder, cone, sphere, torus, closed B-spline) |
| coverage | the STEP pieces of each native edge sum to its arc length (Gauss–Legendre); every native face receives a STEP face |
| faces | every STEP face lies on one native face's surface, with the same surface type, the same outward side, and its non-seam edges among that face's edges; B-splines are checked in both directions |
| adjacency | a STEP edge's two faces map to its native edge's two faces |

The tolerance is 1e-8 m, Parasolid's own `BODY.res_linear`. Every distance is recorded.

## Result

**49/49 models pass.**

| | native | STEP |
|---|---|---|
| faces | 287 | 331 |
| edges | 531 | 688, of which 90 are seams |
| worst vertex distance | | 2.5e-18 m |
| worst edge distance | | 9.0e-18 m |
| worst surface distance | | 3.9e-18 m |
| worst edge-length difference | | 4.2e-17 m |

The deviations are double-precision rounding of the same numbers printed in millimetres. The only
structural difference is seam splitting of closed faces:

- each through-hole cylinder becomes two halves joined by two seams;
- the full sphere becomes two faces;
- the full torus becomes four;
- C20's four trimmed cylinders become eight.

The loft's B-spline surfaces have identical degrees, knots and control points (largest
control-point difference 8.7e-19 m) in both eras. The native loft graph's differences from the
`.x_b` export (EXP-072) therefore do not reach the STEP geometry.

## Controls

The agreement could be an artifact of a loose comparison. Twenty controls, each of which must
fail, were run. All 20 are caught (`controls` in RESULTS.json):

- a vertex moved 1e-7 m;
- a plane offset 1e-7 m;
- a plane tilted 1e-5 rad;
- a face orientation flipped;
- a cylinder, circle, sphere, torus-minor, torus-major or ellipse radius changed by 1e-7 m;
- a cone half-angle changed by 1e-6 rad;
- a B-spline control point moved 1e-6 m;
- a B-spline knot moved;
- a trimmed line shortened by 1e-6;
- a face removed;
- an edge removed;
- four wrong-model pairings: C04/C11, C04/C06, C03/C12 and C00/C18.

The last pairing (cube against split-line cube) originally passed. The split-line edge was being
accepted as a seam. Seams were then restricted to closed surfaces, the documented check above, and
all 49 models still pass.

## Scope

This covers every surface and curve type in the 49 controlled models: plane, cylinder, cone,
sphere, torus, B-spline surface; line, circle, ellipse, trimmed curve. The production parts also
use INTERSECTION curves, edges whose curve is held on their fins, SWEPT_SURF and BLENDED_EDGE. They
have no STEP export, so this experiment does not reach them (NQ-049). Nothing here is cited from
outside this repository.
