# EXP-084 — Reading the online sample, 2007 to 2026

**Question.** Do the converter's readers work on parts nobody in this project made? The test set
is the 141 openly licensed files from EXP-083 (`test files online/`, one folder per save year,
sources and licences in `SOURCES.tsv`).

**Answer.** Yes, after the fixes below. 136 of the files are parts, and all 136 B-reps read to
the terminator with every pointer resolved and 0 graph-check failures. The display mesh reads
on every part that has one. Display and B-rep face counts agree on all but one part, listed under
Open. The census is in `scripts/EXP084/census-online.tsv`. The reader changes are
on the converter's `tool` branch, tested in `test/online.test.js`.

## 1. Sections over 1 MiB come in chunks

A Parasolid section is `u32 size | GUID | chunks | 8 zero bytes`, with each chunk being
`u32 inflated | u32 compressed | zlib`. Most sections have one chunk. A body over 1 MiB is
split into a 1 MiB chunk plus either 4 KiB chunks or one remainder: 1,142,601 bytes in 24
chunks, 1,863,177 bytes in 2. The 2025 bottle holder (`51f01927`, 428 faces) reads only when
the chunks are joined.

The legacy `DisplayLists__ZLB` wrapper uses the same frame without the size field: the GUID,
the chunks, then 8 zero bytes. The 2013 head_back and head_face parts (`82981f2a`,
`f666f342`) have meshes that big, with 532 and 732 faces.

## 2. Node types missing from the 13006 base

Files that use a type the reader didn't know declare it as "same as the base" (declared count
255), so the bytes carry no field list to learn from. The layouts below come from the XT Format
Reference (2006). Each one is checked by traversal: the node ends exactly where the next one
starts, every pointer resolves, and the graph checks pass.

| type | name | fields | seen in |
|---|---|---|---|
| 60 | OFFSET_SURF | geometry, check c, true_offset l, surface p, offset f, scale f | 4 parts |
| 68 | SPUN_SURF | geometry, profile p, base v, axis v, start v, end v, start/end param f, x_axis v, scale f | 3 parts |
| 86 | VECTOR_VALUES | values v[] | 3 parts, see 5 |
| 100 | TRANSFORM | node_id d, owner/next/previous p, rotation f[9], translation v, scale f, flag d, perspective v | 3 parts |
| 163 | HELIX_SU_FORM | axis_pt v, axis_dir v, hand c, turns i, pitch f, gap f, tol f | 4 parts |
| 184 | HELIX_CU_FORM | axis_pt v, axis_dir v, point v, hand c, turns i, pitch f, tol f | same 4 |

Types 85, 87, 88 and 89 (POINT, AXIS, TAG and DIRECTION_VALUES) went in with their reference
layouts but don't occur in any part yet, so they are unverified.

**The helix numbers are swapped compared with the reference.** The 2006 table lists 163 as the
curve helix and 184 as the surface helix. In these files 163 is what a SURFACE_DATA's
`analytic_form` points to (with `analytic_form_type` 'H'), and 184 is what a CURVE_DATA points
to. The bytes agree. In `073482d3` (ReducerBox, 2022) the first 163 node starts at byte 14167
of the partition: type 00a3, declared ff, index 022c, then axis_pt, axis_dir (0,0,1) and
`2b` ('+'), turns [0, 13.75], pitch 0.0008, gap null (the -3.14158e13 marker) and tol 1e-6.
That is 89 bytes of fields, ending where the next node's type (002d) begins. The curve layout,
with its extra `point` vector, would have run 24 bytes past it. Reading 163 with the curve
layout is what produced "malformed new-type declaration -16520 at 14277".

The values make sense as threads: pitches of 0.8 and 1 mm, 13.75 and 5 turns.

## 3. Knot arrays longer than n_knots

Some NURBS keep KNOT_MULT and KNOT_SET arrays longer than `n_knots`, with the spare slots set
to 0 or null. The graph check now accepts those when the spare slots are empty and checks
knot order only on the first `n_knots`. Before this, padded arrays counted as graph failures.

## 4. DisplayLists that hold no tessellation

Five parts have a `Contents/DisplayLists` stream with no faces in it: `06b5063b`, `b32017f5`,
`2693f32a`, `c292e7e2` and `d889c6fc`. Two of them, from different parts, are byte-identical
(12,979 bytes), and their bounding box is the default -1..1. The class names inside are only
scene and UI ones (`uiUserModelEnv_c`, `uiLineFontMgr_c`, `moVisualProperties_c`, lights).

A working stream also carries `uoTempBodyTessData_c` and `uoTempFaceTessData_c`. Across the
125 modern files, the presence of `uoTempBodyTessData_c` matches the presence of faces in
every case: 108 with the class all have faces, 5 without it have none, and 12 have no
DisplayLists at all. These are parts saved without display data. The reader now says so
instead of "No supported face records found". An STL for them has to come from tessellating
the B-rep.

`06b5063b` is a master sketch and has no faces in its B-rep either.

## 5. Cosmetic threads are drawn as separate bands

Three parts had "rejected" face records: `14f9214b` (2), `a0ab30c8` (4) and `4febfc67` (1).
Every one of them has the same shape: two strips of 50 vertices, 100 vertices, normals, then
u32 1 where a face record's Block1 would start. Each one is a closed cylinder band at about a
thread's minor diameter: radius 4.25 mm × 25 mm long, 1.65 mm × 10 mm, and 5.1 mm × 15 mm.
`4febfc67`'s band is followed by the strings "Thread" and "Steel".

The count matches cosmetic threads elsewhere in the file. Each of the three parts has exactly
as many VECTOR_VALUES nodes in its B-rep as it has bands (2, 4, 1), and no other part has
VECTOR_VALUES. The bands are drawn on top of the threaded faces and aren't faces themselves,
so the display face count still equals the B-rep's. The reader lists them as overlays and
leaves them out of the STL.

## 6. Which configuration was active

A part keeps a `Config-N-Partition` for each configuration that was rebuilt. Reading the
lowest N is wrong when the file was saved in another configuration. `c613782b` has
configurations 0 to 3 with 378, 318, 777 and 13 faces, and its display shows 13.

`Header2` is a copy of the active configuration's `Contents/Config-N-ModelHeader`. In
`c613782b` it is byte-identical to Config-3's and to no other. In `cube.SLDPRT` it differs in
one place, the document name ("Part2" against "cube"), so the file was renamed after it was
saved. The reader scores each ModelHeader by the bytes it shares with Header2 at both ends and
takes the best:

- `c613782b`: Config-3 at 1.000, next best 0.874
- `03afebf4` (Profilringe): Config-7 at 1.000, Config-1 at 0.828
- 156 single-configuration parts: the one configuration, all above 0.9

Twelve modern files from before about 2020 have no `Config-N-ModelHeader`. Their Header2
doesn't match any `Contents/Config-N` stream either. For these the reader takes the lowest N.
In the corpus their configurations all have the same face count, but they can still differ in
size: `cut-z071` and `cut-z18` are cut lengths. Comparing the display's bounding box with each
partition's vertices doesn't settle it, because curved parts have no vertex at their extremes.

## 7. Bodies kept in LocalBodies, and which bodies count

`Config-N-FeatureBodies/LocalBodies` holds imported bodies, one per section, with other data
between the sections. `2e6e140a` has an empty partition (609 bytes) and 8 LocalBodies sections of
70, 201, 219, 31, 22, 444, 117 and 13 faces, which add up to its 1,117 display faces. But adding
every LocalBody to the partition overcounts in 6 parts, so something decides which bodies count.

It's an attribute. Every body carries an INT attribute `BODY_IN_LIGHTWEIGHT_PERM`, and a body
with 0 isn't part of the finished part:

- The three pins, `db8a97e2` and `51065319`: their LocalBodies have 0, and the partition solid
  has 1. The display shows only the partition solid. The LocalBodies were imported and then used
  up by a later feature.
- `d0d61cde` (active configuration 1): LocalBodies of 212 and 244 faces have 1, the one of 84
  faces has 0, the partition's 88-face solid has 1, and its four one-face sheets have 0. The
  display's 544 faces are 88 + 212 + 244. The 84-face body shares 128 of its 130 vertices with
  the partition solid (it's the "Sweep1" body's input), and its other two vertices, at z = 59 mm,
  are nowhere in the display.

Counting only bodies whose flag isn't 0 (a missing flag counts as in) gives the display face
count on 187 of 188 parts with a display (`scripts/EXP084/body-flags.txt`). The one miss is
`82981f2a`, which is a mesh question, not a body one (see Open).

A first reading of `d0d61cde` matched 4 + 540 (sheets plus all three LocalBodies), which is also
544. Vertex positions showed that was a coincidence.

The reader leaves out flagged LocalBodies and lists flagged partition bodies so the model builder
skips them. Each section is cut out of a bigger model, so its BODY next/previous and ATTRIB_DEF
next pointers dangle (13 or 14 per section). Each section also numbers its node_ids from 1, so
merged sections get their node_ids moved past the previous maximum, the same way as their indices.

## 8. STL from the B-rep, for parts with no display mesh

Section 4 left 17 parts (5 stubs, 12 with no DisplayLists) with nothing to write an STL from, plus
the 18th, the master sketch. The converter now triangulates the B-rep for them. It samples each
edge once to a chord tolerance (1e-5 m) so both faces share the same points, triangulates each face
in its own (u, v), and splits triangles until each one is within tolerance of the surface.

To see how far that gets, every online part went through it, not only the 18
(`scripts/EXP084/tessellate.js`, results in `tessellate-online.txt`, 120 s per part). Of the 136
parts:

| result | parts |
|---|---|
| closed mesh (every edge used once each way) | 80, one of them the empty master sketch |
| mesh with open or repeated edges | 33: 17 with 35 or fewer, the rest far more |
| throws | 15 |
| over 120 s | 8 |

The closed meshes are accurate. 69 of the 79 with a volume are within 0.1% of the exact B-rep
volume. 71 also have a display mesh, and against that 66 are within 1% (median 0.04%). The
throws come from code that isn't the triangulator:

- 5 × "surfaceInverse: blend". These are blends whose support is itself a blend (head_back,
  head_face, 0739c559, 0132519a, 2f121983). The blend fit in `fit.js` can't project onto an
  unfitted blend.
- 5 × "edge N vertices are N m off its curve" (b32017f5, 0d68d32d, 4803f393, d0d61cde, 51f01927),
  by 28 mm to 210 mm. Resolve trusts the vertices over the curve, and on these it shouldn't.
- 3 × "chain does not end on a cut line", in the seam split (d799e9a3, a3a44709, a4ecc6f5).
- 2 × intersection curves: a march that stops short (d2515121) and tangent surfaces (14f9214b).

For the 18 parts it was written for: 8 give a closed mesh, the master sketch gives an empty one,
6 have open edges, b32017f5 throws and 2 time out.

Three closed meshes disagree with the exact volume, and in each case it's not clear which side is
wrong: `4df87793` (mesh 16.24 mm³, exact 0), `3980555c` (1,819 against 4,914 mm³) and `0eab6495`
(25% over). `065f38b0` is the negative exact volume from Open.

## Open

- **The B-rep triangulation** fails or leaves open edges on 56 of 136 parts (section 8).
- **`d0d61cde` won't resolve.** Its body reads, but an edge (node_id 7, a line between two
  B-spline and plane faces) has vertices 37 mm off its curve, so exact STEP and the B-rep
  triangulation stop there. This happens with the partition alone too.
- **`065f38b0`'s exact volume comes out negative** (−1.04e8 mm³ against a mesh of 5.86e5 mm³),
  so the volume integrator mishandles something in it, probably the thin B-spline ribbon face.
- **`82981f2a` (2013 head_back).** The B-rep has 535 faces and the display has 532. It's an OLE2
  part with 144 OFFSET_SURF nodes. Three faces may have no tessellation.
- **The active configuration in files without ModelHeaders.**
- **Types 85, 87, 88 and 89** are unverified.

## Scripts

All in `scripts/EXP084/`. They take file paths as arguments and load the converter from
`../../../../../solidworks-converters/src`.

| script | what it does |
|---|---|
| `census.js <dir>` | per part: B-rep status, display status, both face counts, rejects |
| `xtdbg.js`, `xthex.js` | last nodes before a parse error; hex and float64 dump of the partition |
| `dldump.js` | writes a part's DisplayLists stream out |
| `tessclass.js <dir>` | tessellation class presence against face count |
| `secprobe.js`, `bodies.js` | every Parasolid section in every stream, with faces per body |
| `sums.js` | which body sum equals the display face count (reads a file list on stdin) |
| `activecfg2.js`, `h2cfg.js`, `cfgname.js`, `bboxcfg.js` | the active-configuration tests in 6 |
| `bodyattr.js` | every body's attributes, per stream |
| `permrule.js`, `bodyrule.js` | the BODY_IN_LIGHTWEIGHT_PERM rule and the earlier vertex rule against the display |
| `tessellate.js` | B-rep triangulation per part: triangles, open and repeated edges, volume against exact and display (section 8; output in `tessellate-online.txt`) |
