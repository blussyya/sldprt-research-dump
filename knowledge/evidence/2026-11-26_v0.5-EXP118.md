# EXP-118: the two holes in head_face's display mesh, and its display edge ids

2026-11-26. The holes are in the file, not a reading error. `f666f342` is the Poppy robot's head face,
a ring-shaped shell saved in SolidWorks 2013 (OLE, 732 faces, one body). Display faces 75 and 448 are
the bores of two screw holes (radius 1.35 mm) through hex bosses, where a cable channel meets each
bore. Their B-rep faces are exact mirror images in x = 0. Each mesh has a closed ring of boundary edges with no edge id, shared with no
other face, up to 1.47 mm from the nearest face. The cause is in the B-rep: the bore face's second
loop is a 96° arc of its bottom circle plus the short top branch of an oval intersection curve. That
loop doesn't go round the bore, and no edge covers the other 264° of the circle, so over 264° the
face has no bottom boundary. They are the only two faces of the 20,501 with edge ids in the corpus
with unlabelled mesh boundary. SolidWorks' own edge labels in the display put each edge on the same
arc and branch as the B-rep does, so two independent records in the file agree.

Separately, the display's edge ids differ from the B-rep's node ids by 0, −1 or +1, constant within
id ranges, on the same 1,810 edges. head_back (`82981f2a`, also 2013) has shifts too, but its display
isn't the same geometry as its B-rep, so the cause there may differ. Both numbers are read straight
from their own fields. No converter output joins edges by id.

Blind checks (2026-11-26, below): both hold, with corrections to the mirror, head_back and the
legacy face id.

Scripts and outputs: `scripts/EXP118/`. No converter change.

The labelled segments sit on the curves they name, which a misread position or id wouldn't do. The

axis. 25 of its 46 vertices are on the bore cylinder; 21 are on no analytic surface within 1e-6 m

and 10 are outside it (`rim.out`).

## What the B-rep says

Native face 2516 (cylinder, radius 1.35 mm, the bore): loops `1165 | 1166 1093` (`hole_vs_edges.out`,
`edge1093.out`).

- 1165: a full circle at −4.300 mm, shared with plane 2474 (the top of the hex boss).
- 1166: a circle at −6.200 mm, vertices 1908 (V1) and 1861 (V2), edge and coedge in the curve's
  direction. From V1 to V2 the circle runs 96.2° (`spans.out`, three points along it at 336.0, 311.9
  and 287.9° from V1). Shared with plane 2502.
- 1093: an intersection curve (the bore and blend 2427, the cable channel), the same two vertices,
  edge in the curve's direction, coedge reversed. The curve is closed: its start and end points are
  both V1, and its 33 chart points run V1, down to −6.31 mm, back up to −6.20 near V2, down to
  −8.20 and back to V1 (`facts.out`). Running from V1 to V2 in its own direction, the edge is the
  short top branch (−6.20 to −6.31 mm), over the same 96.2° as 1166.

So loop 1 is the 96° arc out and the short branch back: it encloses a sliver and goes round the bore
0 times (`loopB.out`). With loop 0 going round once, the face has a top and no bottom over the other
264°. No native edge has a point within 1e-5 m of that 264° arc (`longarc.out`, one point of 1093
at V2). A bore through a plate would have the 264° arc there, and the face's second loop would be the
264° arc plus the short branch; that is what the mesh draws, without ids.

SolidWorks' display agrees with this reading, not with that one. Display face 72 (plane 2502) and
face 75 both label 1166 on 8 segments over the 96.2° between V1 and V2, at −6.200 mm; faces 56 (the
blend) and 75 both label 1093 on 23 segments over the same 96.2°, at −6.20 to −6.31 mm
(`spans.out`). Those labels are SolidWorks' tessellation of its own body, read without the B-rep
reader. If the B-rep reader had a circle's direction or an edge's vertices backwards here, the
labels would sit on the other arc.

`face75-unrolled-full.png` shows it: the mesh (orange) stops at a ragged red ring near the blue line
at −6.2 mm. The labelled sliver sits between the two squares, and the rest of the green oval
(1093's curve) lies below the mesh, where no face has triangles.

## The mirror: face 448

Native face 12990, loops `11111 | 11124 11135`, the same three edge types. Face 448's labels are
11112, 11125 and 11136, one more than the native edges they lie on: 11112 is on circle 11111 within
6.6e-10 m and 11125 on circle 11124 within 6.1e-10 m (`mirror448.out`, measured on the mirror images of
1165 and 1166). That explains why the EXP-115 agent couldn't join face 448 by id: see the next
section. Its ring is 40 segments at −5.92 to −6.33 mm.

The B-rep faces are exact mirrors: 0 m difference on radius, axis, circle centres, vertices and all
33 chart points (prove agent, `prove/claim3.out`). So are the 55 labelled boundary vertices of each
display face. The unlabelled rings aren't: 46 against 40 segments, up to 3.05e-4 m apart, and only
face 75's mesh leaves its stored box (by 0.18 mm, disprove agent).

## Display edge ids off by one

For every display edge label, the native edges with id label − k (k from −12 to 12) whose two end
vertices both lie on the label's segments within 1e-6 m (`offset_ends.out`):

| labels | offset (label − native id) |
|---|---|
| up to 9087 | 0 (841 labels) |
| 9541 to 9601 | −1 (6) |
| from 10899 | +1 (858) |

1,705 of 1,810 labels match one edge, 42 match several, 63 none (closed edges, which have no
vertices). The B-rep has 1,810 edges and the display 1,810 labels. The shifts happen in id ranges
that hold no edge: native ids 9088 to 9541 and 9603 to 10897 are surfaces, curves, points,
attributes and type-133 nodes (`offset_step.out`). So the two numberings count the same edges and
differ in how many other nodes sit between them.

Neither Poppy file has another B-rep copy to compare against: the only GUID stream with faces is
Config-0-Partition, section 0 (`ghost.out`). Both numbers are read from their own fields: Block1's
u32 per edge, and each node's `node_id`. Nothing in the converter joins display edges to native
edges by id. The face join would, but these OLE records use the legacy grammar and carry no face id.

*Corrected 2026-11-26 (disprove agent):*

- The legacy records do carry a face id. The u32 at offset 82 of the legacy tail is shifted the same
  way: face 75 stores 2516 and face 448 stores 12991, against native 12990. On the 96-byte tails it
  reads a native face id, or one more, on 357 of 732 faces; the other 375 tails hold more data
  before that point (checked in the session).
- head_back matched without an id window shifts by 0, −1, −4, −9 and −44, the opposite sign to
  `f666f342`. Its display has 532 faces against 535 native, with 36 corners 0.1 to 1.18 mm from any
  native vertex, so its display isn't the same geometry as its B-rep (`disprove/p13_82981f2a.out`).

## Chainwheel, for the record

The question was whether the converter now finds and fixes such cases itself. It does, by rule: a
control-0 strip's ids are read in their own order (EXP-117, commit 894360c). That isn't a guess per
file. Over the corpus (`boundary_corpus.out`): 216 part files, 20,519 display faces, 14 without
boundary cycles, all in `plate4` with "No edge table in this layout". Every face that has edge ids
closes. My walk skipped `test files new/re-exports`. Both agents' sweeps include its 5 parts (221
files, 20,567 faces) and find the same.

## Blind checks (2026-11-26)

Two agents with their own record reader, strip walk and winding (`scripts/EXP118/prove/`,
`scripts/EXP118/disprove/`).

- **The holes are in the file: holds.** Both find one closed chain per face (46 and 40 edges,
  every vertex of degree 2, Block1 id 0, Block3 byte 0), shared by no other face, 1.48e-7 and
  1.48e-6 m at the closest. Records are read whole: an own scan gives the same 732 records and
  identical triangles; a scan of every byte offset of the 4.24 MB DisplayLists stream finds no bore
  vertex outside the parsed faces (control: it finds all 103 and 94 on the two faces). Controls:
  one deleted triangle adds 3 unlabelled edges; one cleared id is caught, 411 of 411 over the corpus.
- **The labels sit on their curves: holds for face 75, fails as stated for face 448.** Face 448's
  labels name edges 4 to 6 mm away and lie on the edges one id lower (the shift above). 1093's labels
  are on its chart within 2.07e-5 m (chordal error 8.7e-5 m) and 1.05 mm from the other branch;
  against the exact cylinder × blend, 14 of 24 vertices are within 1e-7 m and the worst is 8.8e-6 m.
- **The loop: holds.** Winding +1 for the ring, 0 for the second loop, on both faces. Flipping the
  circle gives a valid face; flipping 1093 still gives 0, since its oval doesn't go round the axis.
  SolidWorks' labels cover 100% of the branches the reader chose and 1 to 2% of the others. No edge
  lies along the other 264° (34 to 51 trimmed curves unsampled, all 15.96 mm or more away).
  Corpus control: of 8,293 cylinder and cone faces in 215 parts, only these two have loops like
  this. At the two other 1.35 mm bores of the part (x = ±20 mm) the same arc and intersection bound
  a face of their own (3713, 14655).
- **The only two in the corpus: holds.** 221 files, 197 with a display, 20,567 faces.
- **Side finding.** 11 of 74,026 triangles in the file face against their stored normals; 6 are
  slivers in faces 75 and 448 next to the ring.

What changed: face 448's labels are one id higher than the edges they lie on; the mirror holds for
the B-rep and the labelled boundary only; head_back's shifts are 0 to −44 and its display differs
from its B-rep; the legacy tail does hold a face id. The sibling bores suggest the arc-and-
intersection loop belongs to a face of its own here too, which is a guess.
