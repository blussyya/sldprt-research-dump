## 1. A selection method that does not use a walker

EXP-061 located the bounding record via `v0.2`'s face offsets; EXP-062 via its own walker. Both
select candidates by recognising array headers. This experiment selects by **arithmetic alone**:
every byte offset in the stream is tested, and a position is accepted only if the ten `f64` at
`+12..+91` satisfy

```
centre == (min + max) / 2        on all three axes, <= 1e-12
radius == |max - centre|                            <= 1e-12
```

with a radius floor. No parser, no walker, no header assumption.

**Ordering (`min <= max`) is deliberately not a selection criterion.** EXP-062's countermodel is
that swapping the min/max triples preserves both identities, so the identities alone cannot
assign the fields. Leaving ordering out of selection turns it into a real test.

> **Language corrected 2026-09-21 (EXP-065).** This section originally called the 978,357 hits
> "denormal garbage". They are nothing of the kind: those offsets hold **small integers** —
> counts, indices and array headers — read at 8-byte width. At `C00`'s first face the values are
> int32 `12, 100, 2`, the documented `[12,100,2,V]` positions header. 52.8% of all offsets in that
> stream decode as tiny-nonzero `f64` because the stream is mostly integer data. The radius floor
> remains the right fix; the reason is that those regions are integers, not that they are noise.

### The first run of this scan was worthless, and that is the control

Selecting on the identities with a plain finiteness check returned **978,357 records**. The
identities are trivially satisfied when every decoded value is a denormal around `1e-300`:
`|C - (A+B)/2| <= 1e-12` says nothing without an absolute scale. This is the same defect as the
stubbed `'area': 0` quantity that produced spurious matches in EXP-061, and the same defect this
project identified in an outside project's "finite f32" reading.

Adding a radius floor of 1e-9 m removes all of them. The failed run is retained because it
supplies the discriminating control for §2:

| selection | records | `min <= max` |
|---|---:|---:|
| identities only (denormal garbage admitted) | 978,357 | 337,697 (34.5%) |
| identities + radius floor | 1,461 | **1,461 (100%)** |

Random data satisfying the identities is **unordered about two-thirds of the time**. Real records
are unordered **never**.

## 2. The layout is confirmed, and the ordering gap EXP-062 identified is now closed

Across 45 modern files, **1,461 records, 1,461 ordered, zero unordered**. Selection could not
have produced this; it is measured after the fact against a background that is 65% unordered.

EXP-062 is right that the two identities do not by themselves exclude the swap. The swap is
excluded **empirically**, not logically, and that is the correct standing for it. EXP-061's
wording — "a wrong assignment cannot satisfy both simultaneously" — was too strong for the one
symmetry that can; it is sound against every other misassignment.

Reconciled against the parser on the SW2022 models: **142/142** faces found walker-free.

(Three faces initially failed — `C16` f0, `C17` f0/f1 — and the cause was our filter, not the
data. They carry coordinate residues of `4.34e-19` where the true value is 0, which a "zero or
>= 1e-9" test rejects. Their identities hold at exactly 0.0. The lower bound belongs on the
radius, not on coordinates.)

## 3. EXP-062 is right about the byte inventory; EXP-061 was wrong

Checked by span arithmetic rather than argument:

```
decoded doubles            10 x f64
byte span they occupy      12..91 inclusive
unclaimed bytes inside it  none (contiguous)
  EXP-061 called +32  undecoded -> INSIDE the f64 at +28
  EXP-061 called +56  undecoded -> INSIDE the f64 at +52
  EXP-061 called +80  undecoded -> INSIDE the f64 at +76
  EXP-061 called +88  undecoded -> INSIDE the f64 at +84
```

The ten doubles are contiguous from 12 to 91. EXP-061's "undecoded" list was generated by a
stride-4 scan over offsets absent from the decoded list, without checking whether they were
already spanned. Every one of those four was the upper half of a double we had already read.
The genuine unknowns are **`+0..+11` and `+92..+131`**.

### What is actually in them

`+0..+11` is **constant**: `00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 01 00 00 00` — three `u32` LE reading `0, 0,
1` — on **1,414/1,414** face records. EXP-061 reported these as "`0 0 1` on the cube's face 0,
matching no known count" and went looking for a count. There is no count: the field does not vary
at all. Per INV-022's standing caution, constant data supports no semantic inference.

`+92..+131` is nonzero on every record. Read as `u32`, `+128` is `1` on 1,414/1,414 and `+96` on
1,380; `+100` takes 1,416 distinct values across 1,461 records, so it is near-unique per record
and is not a length under any `f64` reading. Not decoded here.

## 4. A second record class: one bounding box per configuration

The walker-free scan finds **47 records that are not face records** — 1,461 against the parser's
1,414. They are not false positives. Each is immediately preceded by a configuration name in
UTF-16LE, and each carries that configuration's overall box:

```
@16365 preceding: ".%....e...........................D.e.f.a.u.l.t....."
       min 0.000e+0,0.000e+0,0.000e+0  max 1.000e-2,1.000e-2,1.000e-2
       == union of that file's face boxes?  true
```

The USB-hub files carry the Russian default configuration name `По умолчанию` in the same
position. Across the corpus:

| measure | result |
|---|---|
| modern files carrying at least one | **45/45** |
| records per file | 1 in 43 files, 2 in 2 files |
| box == union of that file's face boxes | **45/47** |

The two non-matching records are exactly the two files carrying two records — multi-configuration
files, where the union of all face boxes spans both configurations. Same 132-byte layout,
different scope. This is a new record class, not previously recorded.

## 5. NQ-045 — RESOLVED. The record is in the legacy container, unchanged

The legacy geometry stream is `DisplayLists__ZLB` and `parser/v0.2` rejects legacy files outright,
so there is no walker to be circular with. The same walker-free scan, applied to the inflated
legacy stream:

| check | result |
|---|---|
| SolidWorks 2011 OLE2 files | 25 |
| `DisplayLists` stream located | **25/25** |
| stream inflated | **25/25** |
| files yielding at least one bounding record | **25/25** |
| bounding records found | **145** |
| of which `min <= max` on all axes | **145/145** |

The boxes are correct against dimensions known in advance from the build specification, in metres:

| model | decoded box | nominal |
|---|---|---|
| `C00_cube_10mm` | `[0,0,0] .. [0.01,0.01,0.01]` | 10 mm cube at origin |
| `C01_cube_20mm` | `[0,0,0] .. [0.02,0.02,0.02]` | 20 mm cube |
| `C02_cube_translated` | `[0.05,0.05,0] .. [0.06,0.06,0.01]` | 10 mm cube moved 50 mm |
| `C14_sphere` | `[-0.005]³ .. [0.005]³` | r = 5 mm |
| `C15_torus` | `[-0.007,-0.002,-0.007] .. [0.007,0.002,0.007]` | R = 5 mm, r = 2 mm |
| `C19_half_cylinder` | `[-0.005,0,-0.005] .. [0.005,0.01,0]` | half of r5 × 10 mm |

Record counts equal native face counts on every model checked against the build log — `C00` 6,
`C04` 7, `C10` 11, `C13` 2, `C14` 1, `C15` 1, `C17` 3.

Cross-era, on the geometry-matched models: **legacy box == modern box on 21/22**, to 1e-6 m.

**INV-026 is therefore a format property, not a SolidWorks 2022 property.** It holds across a
complete container change. It also means analytic per-face geometry is readable out of 2011 files
**today**, with no legacy DisplayLists parser — the same conclusion EXP-060 reached for the
Parasolid layer, now extended to the tessellation container.

### This independently confirms EXP-063's C16 counterexample

The one cross-era disagreement is `C16_loft_spline`, and it is the model EXP-063 flagged. The
stored box is padded outside the nominal geometry in **both** eras, by different amounts:

| era | padding on all six sides |
|---|---|
| SolidWorks 2011 | ~7.0 µm |
| SolidWorks 2022 | **1.036515329e-5 m** |

The 2022 figure is EXP-063's 10.365153 µm, recovered here with no OpenCascade, no STEP file and
no external dependency — a third independent route to the same number. Per-face, that exact
offset appears on all four spline faces; the planar cap `f0` carries a different one
(`1.041666e-5`). EXP-063's caution is correct and now rests on two independent methods: **stored
boxes are not universally tight**, and the enlargement is spline-specific and version-dependent.

## 6. Correction: EXP-060's refutation of the node-count reading is withdrawn

EXP-060 concluded the `Z+3` field "is not a node or topology count", on the grounds that
`C04_cube_hole_5mm` and `C06_cube_hole_moved` have identical topology but read 316 and 293.

That inference does not survive the legacy corpus. Grouping models by topology:

| group | SW2011 | SW2022 |
|---|---|---|
| cube (C00, C01, C02) | **constant 251** | **constant 232** |
| cube + 1 hole (C04, C05, C06, C11) | **constant 297** | varies 316 / 293 |
| cube + 2 holes (C07, C08) | **constant 357** | **constant 350** |
| fillet (C03, C12) | **constant 319** | **constant 315** |

In the legacy corpus the field is constant within **every** equal-topology group — invariant to
dimension *and* to position, which is exactly how a topology-determined count must behave. In the
modern corpus it is constant in 3 of 4 groups.

The remaining group is the one EXP-060 drew its conclusion from, and the comparison was the wrong
one. `C05` (3 mm hole), `C06` (5 mm hole moved) and `C11` (4 mm hole) **all read 293** — they
differ from one another in both hole size and position. `C04` is a lone outlier at 316. The build
log records identical volume (`803.650459` mm³) and identical build for C04 and C06, so nothing
in the corpus explains it.

So the evidence is not "moving a feature changes the field". It is "the field is
topology-determined in 22/22 legacy and 21/22 modern models, with one unexplained modern
outlier". **The node-count reading of NQ-043 is live again**, as EXP-062 argued on separate
grounds when it noted that equivalent topology may still serialize different node sets.

EXP-060's other NQ-043 result stands and is re-verified: `binary − text` is 0 on **25/25** legacy
pairs, against the face count on 24/24 modern pairs. That relation is version-specific, as
EXP-062 says.
