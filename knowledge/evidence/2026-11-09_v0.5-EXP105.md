# EXP-105: why display vertices sit off curved faces

The off-surface vertices on cylinder and cone faces are shared boundary vertices. They come from a
neighbouring face's mesh and sit on straight chords of this face's boundary, not on its surface.
Every other vertex on those faces is within 1e-9 m of the joined surface (max 9.8e-10 m). The
radius field, units, transforms and staleness are ruled out.

Measured by a Sonnet agent with its own closed-form distances (`scripts/EXP105/`, `findings.md`).
No blind prove/disprove pass.

## Results (measured)

| part | off vertices (over 1e-9 m) | collinear with 2 others of their face | shared with another display face |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `8cd79ff5` | 619 of 2,419 | 619 | 619 |
| `2f121983` | 987 of 3,403 | 814 | 1,057 (wrong joins included) |
| `a4ecc6f5` | 2,338 of 4,586 | 1,439 | 2,598 (wrong joins included) |

- Example: `8cd79ff5` face 1462 (a cylinder, r 3.24 mm). 10 of 14 vertices sit at 1e-11 m, and 4 sit 1.10e-5 m inward, in pairs, one per rim, at one angle.
- Control: C13 (a cone alone) has 144 vertices, max 1.8e-10 m, none off. C04 and C19 also give none. C20 (crossed cylinders) and C21 (a cylinder fillet) reproduce the effect: 20 and 66 off, all on chords and shared.
- EXP-091 found 1e-9 m on its faces because they had no curved neighbour. This also explains EXP-097's boundary vertices up to 6.9e-4 m off, and the 0.3% residual EXP-100 and EXP-099 saw.
- Staleness, ModelStamps against DLUpdateStamp: 129/128 (`8cd79ff5`), 128/128, 4165/4165, 106/106 (C13). The current parts show the effect too.

## What it means for checks

A display-to-surface distance check has to leave out vertices shared with other faces, or test
them against the boundary edge instead. Otherwise curved faces fail a 1e-8 m bar for reasons that
have nothing to do with the surface.
