# EXP-105: why display vertices sit off curved faces

The off-surface vertices on cylinder and cone faces are shared boundary vertices. They come from a
neighbouring face's mesh and sit on straight chords of this face's boundary, not on its surface.
Every other vertex on those faces is within 1e-9 m of the joined surface (max 9.8e-10 m). The
radius field, units, transforms and staleness are ruled out.

Measured by a Sonnet agent with its own closed-form distances (`scripts/EXP105/`, `findings.md`).

Blind checks (2026-11-23, below): the mechanism holds, the numbers as stated don't. The 1e-9 m bar
is below float32 rounding for any part larger than a few centimetres (rounding alone puts a point
5e-9 m off a cylinder at 0.1 m from the origin), so "every other vertex within 1e-9 m" fails across
the corpus. At 1e-7 m, all 23,700 off vertices in 44 parts are boundary vertices; 41 aren't on a
chord and 27 aren't shared with another face.

- Example: `8cd79ff5` face 1462 (a cylinder, r 3.24 mm). 10 of 14 vertices sit at 1e-11 m, and 4 sit 1.10e-5 m inward, in pairs, one per rim, at one angle.
- Control: C13 (a cone alone) has 144 vertices, max 1.8e-10 m, none off. C04 and C19 also give none. C20 (crossed cylinders) and C21 (a cylinder fillet) reproduce the effect: 20 and 66 off, all on chords and shared.
- EXP-091 found 1e-9 m on its faces because they had no curved neighbour. This also explains EXP-097's boundary vertices up to 6.9e-4 m off, and the 0.3% residual EXP-100 and EXP-099 saw.
- Staleness, ModelStamps against DLUpdateStamp: 129/128 (`8cd79ff5`), 128/128, 4165/4165, 106/106 (C13). The current parts show the effect too.

## What it means for checks

A display-to-surface distance check has to leave out vertices shared with other faces, or test
them against the boundary edge instead. Otherwise curved faces fail a 1e-8 m bar for reasons that
have nothing to do with the surface.

## Blind checks (2026-11-23)

Two agents with their own closed-form cylinder and cone distances and none of this file's scripts
(`scripts/EXP105/prove/`, `scripts/EXP105/disprove/`). Display and B-rep are in the same unit and
frame in both.

**The bar.** Display positions are float32. Points placed exactly on a cylinder or cone and rounded
to float32 miss it by 6e-10 m at 0.01 m from the origin, 5e-9 at 0.1 m and 8e-8 at 1 m; in float64
the same points are 1e-17 off (disprove, `control_f32.out`). So a 1e-9 m bar fails on float32
rounding alone, and this file's "within 1e-9 m" only held because its three parts are small.

**Prove: fails as stated, holds on some parts.** 148,717 vertices on 4,965 cylinder and cone faces
in 75 files. 41,705 are over 1e-9 m in 54 files; 22,193 are beyond 1e-9 m plus float32 rounding.
Of those, 15,831 fit the claim (boundary, shared, on a chord at 1e-9 m), 2,715 aren't boundary, 91
aren't shared, 5,387 aren't on a chord at that tolerance and 884 weren't tested. Every over-bar
vertex fits on `8cd79ff5`, C20, C21 and five online parts. Controls: synthetic points on a
cylinder and a cone give 8.7e-18; moves down to 2e-9 m are caught; a tampered C13 vertex moved
1e-6 m reads 8.9e-7 (1e-6 times the cosine of the half angle).

**Disprove: partly holds.** At 1e-7 m: 23,700 off vertices in 44 parts, all on the boundary, 0
interior. 23,659 lie on the straight chord between their boundary neighbours. 23,609 are shared
exactly with another face, and 90 more within 1e-8 m. Counterexamples:
- 41 off the chord: 15 in `2f121983` (edges 26 and 521, 0.1 to 0.3 µm *outside* the surface, which a
  chord of a convex arc can't give), 22 in `d0d61cde` (up to 9.9e-7), 2 in `a4ecc6f5`, 2 in Dekor.
- 27 with no vertex of another face within 1e-5 m: 26 in BME280 (`03764871`), 1 in `3c4752f1`.
Controls: on C21 a 5 µm radial shift of each off vertex fails the chord test every time; on-surface
boundary vertices pass it 0.8% of the time (straight edges); C13 has no off vertex.

**Why the agents' interior counts differ (checked by me).** Prove took the boundary from the
converter's `boundaryCycles`, disprove from every vertex on an edge with a nonzero Block1 ID. On
faces where the converter can't build cycles ("Ambiguous boundary graph", seam faces) prove's
boundary set is empty, so every off vertex there counted as interior. Measured: 24 such faces in
`ef1d5cfe` hold 3,789 edge vertices that aren't in any cycle, 1 face in `0132519a` holds 268; on
`8cd79ff5`, `a4ecc6f5` and `d0d61cde` the two sets are identical (`scripts/EXP105/reconcile/`).
`2f121983` has 9 faces with no cycles, which probably holds its 267, not checked vertex by vertex.
So the disprove reading stands: off-surface vertices beyond float32 rounding are boundary vertices.

**Corrected claim.** On cylinder and cone faces, display vertices further off the surface than
float32 rounding are boundary vertices. Nearly all lie on the chord between their boundary
neighbours and are shared with the neighbouring face. Exceptions: 41 off-chord and 27 unshared
vertices in five parts, not explained. A surface-distance check on display vertices needs a
per-vertex float32 bound, not a fixed 1e-9 m, and has to leave the boundary out.

**Also found.** The converter can't build boundary cycles on faces with a seam (both fins on one
face), 34 faces in the three parts above.
