# EXP-119: why head_face didn't resolve in 2,400 s (NQ-069)

2026-11-26. A stop test that can't pass. On a B-surface with a short parameter direction, the
Gauss-Newton foot settles in 3 steps and then jitters by about 2e-15 in that parameter. The stop
test asks for a step under 1e-15 × (1 + |u|) in parameter space, so it never passes. After 30
steps Newton reports failure, and the caller searches the whole surface on a grid for a point it
already had. On `f666f342` that happens thousands of times inside nested blend spines. With the stop
measured in space instead (the unclamped step moves the point less than 1e-14 m), the same spine
takes 55 s instead of 189 to 204 s and comes out within 8.8e-17 m of the old one. The whole part
resolves for the first time, in 3,214 s. It isn't clean: 19 edges and 2 blend surfaces are over
1e-8 m, the worst an SP_CURVE 4.2 cm from its vertices (next section).

Scripts: `scripts/EXP119/` (`timing.js`, `stuck.js`, `nested.js`), outputs next to them. The
instrumented copies of the evaluator used to count paths are described below and weren't kept.
Converter change: `src/geom/eval.js`, `bsplineNewton`.

## Where the time went

`timing.js` logs every blend-surface and edge fit as it starts and ends. Before the change, the
first 30 fits took 81 s, then face 13077's blend took 461 s, and faces 13121, 14137 and 14364 took
1,133, 560 and 643 s (`timing-f666f342.log`; other probes ran at the same time, so these are
inflated). `stuck.js` timed the evaluator by function on face 13077: `surfaceInverse` was 238.7 of
246.6 s, 1.1 ms a call over 210,643 calls (`stuck-13077.out`).

Face 13077's blend (radius 2.5 mm) rolls on a zero-radius blend and on blend 15483. Blend 15483's
spine runs on blend 15480, whose spine lies on B-surface 15500: 3 × 96 control points, degree 1 × 3,
u from 0 to 1.01 over 1.5 mm. Fitting blend 15483's spine alone took 242 s (`nested-13077.out`).

Counting the paths in a copy of the evaluator, over 44 s of that fit: 1,758 calls on surface 15500
where Newton from the warm start failed and a grid search followed. In 949 of them the point was on
the surface (within 1e-6 m). Stepping one of those by hand: Newton reached the grid's answer in 3
steps (u 0.005462248701820, v 0.430136547634), then stepped by 2e-15 to 9.5e-15 in u for the
remaining 27. With u near 0.0055 the test needs under 1e-15. Each grid search evaluates the surface
9,625 times.

## The change

The offset path already stopped when a step moved the point less than 1e-14 m (EXP-103). Plain feet
didn't, because a stop like that once put the spring `5f8e9db3`'s feet on the wrong coil. That stop
measured the step after clamping it to the knot range, and at a knot edge the clamped step is zero
while the foot is still moving. The new test measures the unclamped step, so it stops only where
Newton has settled. For offsets the two are the same, since their step isn't clamped.

| check | result |
|---|---|
| blend 15483's spine, old evaluator, alone with the sweep running | 189.4 s and 204.0 s |
| the same, new evaluator | 54.7 s |
| the two spines | 213 control points each, error 9.315e-10 m each; control points within 8.8e-17 m, curve points within 7.7e-17 m |
| fits finished in both full runs (167) | same error on 167 of 167 |
| grid searches after the change, same 44 s | 1,321, all for points 0.1 mm or more off the surface (chord samples between the 11 chart points of blend 15480's spine) |
| `npm test` | 86 pass, 2 skipped |
| whole part | resolves in 3,214 s (`timing-f666f342-after.log`) |

Regression sweep over the online sample against EXP-103's final sweep (`sweep-after.txt`): nothing
worse. head_back, the spring and `2f121983` give the same numbers. On the 101 parts that resolve in
both, the total time went from 1,325 to 1,140 s (`sweep-times.out`): Carter2019 48 to 1 s,
`073482d3` 115 to 20 s, the spring `5f8e9db3` 50 to 8 s, `a4ecc6f5` 86 to 47 s. The after-run shared
the machine with other jobs, so these are upper bounds. The spring's slowdown from EXP-103's dense
check (NQ-070, 19 to 50 s) is gone. head_face took 2,931 s. Converter commit ab2214a.

The part is mirrored, and so are the numbers: 1539 against 11178/11180 (2.44e-3 m), 2664 against
13194. The largest gap between a tolerant edge's curve and its other face is 12,520 m, which means
something evaluated far outside its range, not a near miss. Exact STEP for this part shouldn't be
trusted until these are explained.
