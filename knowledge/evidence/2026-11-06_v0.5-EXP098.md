# EXP-098: the converter picks the configuration from the display

The converter now reads the configuration whose B-rep matches the saved display, in parts with
several configurations and no ModelHeader. That fixes the two parts where the lowest ID was wrong:
`118a3cb0` now reads config 268 (the lowest was 56 mm off) and `289756ce` reads 21 (0.1 mm off).
When nothing matches, it keeps the lowest ID, as before.

The rule fits the display. It doesn't prove a shape: two blind checks found it right in every corpus
part, and found it can tie configurations that differ inside the box (below).

Converter: `solidworks-converters` `dev` at 9d39a6c, tie-breaker at 517476c (`src/parasolid/select.js`, `readBody` in
`src/parasolid/partition.js`). Scripts and outputs: `scripts/EXP098/`.

## The rule (2026-11-06)

A configuration matches when both hold:

- its native B-rep, assembled as the export assembles it (first partition section, in-part
  LocalBodies, hidden bodies left out), has as many faces as the display. This is the count before
  the STEP export splits periodic faces; the exported STEP has more (18 against 26, 92 against 104);
- its box equals the union of the display's per-face stored bounds within 1e-8 m.

The B-rep box is every native vertex, single-vertex loops included, plus every edge's extremes
after procedural curves are fitted. Lines, circles and ellipses give exact extremes. Other curves
give 257 samples. Several matches take the lowest; EXP-094 and EXP-097 found tied configurations
export the same STEP. That holds in the four tied corpus parts. It isn't a property of the test
(see the blind check).

Two things changed from the first version of the code, both found on `300f9732`:

- Intersection curves have to be fitted before the box (the resolver the STEP export uses).
  Without that, the box code threw and the part fell back without being measured.
- Arcs got exact extremes instead of samples. That didn't change `300f9732` (below), but samples
  bound a box from inside, so they can only cause misses.

## Why `300f9732` doesn't match (measured)

Config 0 equals the display box on five sides. On +z it's 2.5e-8 m short. The display, not the
B-rep, is off:

- The B-rep has four tolerant vertices (tolerance 1e-8 m), at y = 0.061 m and y = 0.215 m.
- The display faces at those two y values are padded: 5e-8 m in x and y, 2.5e-8 m in z. Display
  faces 47 and 55 run over the same 2 mm arcs and their top is exactly the arc's, 0.128522770956 m.
- Config 1 has 46 faces against the display's 92.

So the fallback reads config 0, which is right here because it's the only configuration with the
display's face count. The bar stays at 1e-8 m. Why the padding is 5e-8 m for a 1e-8 m tolerance is
not checked yet.

> **Corrected 2026-11-06:** this first said 152 of 186. The 186 counted the three parts that hit
> the time limit, which weren't measured. Found when the disprove check reported 183 testable.

Of the 31 gaps:

- 29 have the B-rep box inside the display box. The SW2011 sphere, torus and loft samples are
  among them: those faces bulge past their edges, and the box only looks at edges and vertices.
- 19 have tolerant vertices or edges.
- 7 are under 1 µm.
- 2 have the B-rep outside the display. `636b653d` has eight vertices 2.794 mm past the display's
  lowest y with equal face counts; `f0b9a118` (16 tolerant vertices) is 0.26 mm outside.
- `eefa906d` is the largest, 0.325 m inside, on a 6-face part.

Every miss is a false negative. On a multi-configuration part a false negative falls back to the
lowest ID, the old rule. That is no worse than before, but no better either: the lowest ID is 89%
off in volume on `118a3cb0`. The rule picks wrong if the right configuration misses while a wrong
one matches, or if two configurations share face count and box but differ inside. Neither happened
in the nine parts.

## Blind check (2026-11-06)

Both ran on Sonnet, with their own scripts, after a container restart stopped the first runs.
Both say partly holds.

Prove (`scripts/EXP098/prove/`, own box: 1500 samples per curved edge with per-axis refinement and
analytic circle extremes):

- Same fits on all seven parts without ModelHeader: 268; 21; {0,1,4}; {0,1,4}; {0,1}; {0,1}; none
  for `300f9732` (2.5e-8 m). Both controls pass; in `03afebf4` config 1 has the same 7 faces and
  only the box (1.3 mm) rules it out.
- Tied configurations export identical STEP (timestamp normalised, same md5) in all four parts.
- Found the claim's wording wrong: the face count that matches is the native one, not the
  exported STEP's. The code already used the native count. The rule above is reworded.
- The seven parts have no ground truth: the check shows fit, not that the configuration was active.

Disprove (`scripts/EXP098/disprove/`):

- Mesh volume against B-rep volume, a second witness: 268 is 0.04% off (next best 1.1%), 21 is
  0.09% off (config 1, 1.5%). Configurations outside the ties are 36% to 440% off.
- A hole in the test (`synth.js`): six single-configuration cubes (fillet, chamfer, 3, 4 and 5 mm
  holes, moved hole) all pass against the 5 mm cube's display, with 7 faces and gaps of 1.7e-18 m or
  less. Their STEP differs. The rule would take the first, a cube of 998 mm³ against 804 mm³. Mesh
  volume separates all six.
- Single-configuration control: 156 of 183 match with its sampler, against 152 of 183 with the
  converter's. The four parts it gains are probably spline extremes the 257 samples miss; which four
  is not checked yet.
- The rule changes the converter's answer in two of seven parts. The case for it rests on those two
  and the two controls.

What changed because of them: the face-count wording, the miss count, and the claim now says the
tie rule is a corpus fact. The converter is unchanged; a stronger tie check is the next step.

## Tie-breaker (2026-11-07)

Ties are now ranked by the display mesh (`dev` 517476c, `meshResidual` in `select.js`). Per display
face, up to 8 mesh vertices are checked against each B-rep surface for distance and for the angle
between the surface normal and the stored mesh normal. A face takes the surface that keeps its
vertices within 1e-8 m with the smallest worst angle; the configuration scores its worst face.
Equal scores keep the lowest ID.

Positions alone weren't enough. The first version used only distance and picked the fillet cube
for the chamfer cube's display: a flat chamfer is meshed from its four corners, and those lie on the
1 mm fillet's cylinder too. The chamfer's mesh normals are 0.79 rad off the cylinder's there. The
first version also scored `afc71d14`'s own geometry at 2.03 mm, because it took the three surfaces
nearest a face's first vertex, usually a corner where several surfaces meet. Both fixed.

Measured (`scripts/EXP098/tie-break.js`, `tie-break.json`):

- Control: on the SW2011 fillet and chamfer cubes every mesh normal is within 2.2e-7 rad of the
  part's own surfaces. The normals are unit float32 vectors.
- Six cubes that tie on face count and box (fillet, chamfer, 3, 4 and 5 mm holes, moved hole),
  offered against each cube's display in turn: 6 of 6 pick their own cube. The right one scores
  2.2e-10 to 6.0e-10 m and at most 3.9e-7 rad; the closest wrong one is 0.29 mm or 0.79 rad off.
- The four tied corpus parts score exactly equal across each tie, so they keep the lowest ID
  (unchanged): `629adcec`, `70477058`, `516a7dc5`, `afc71d14`, at 1.9e-10 to 1.7e-9 m.
- Converter tests: `test/select.test.js`; `npm test` 66 pass, 0 fail, 2 skipped (no OCP).

Not checked: the tie-breaker hasn't been through blind agents, and no corpus part has a tie between
different shapes, so the cubes are its only positive test.
