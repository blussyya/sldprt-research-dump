# EXP-098: the converter picks the configuration from the display

The converter now reads the configuration whose B-rep matches the saved display, in parts with
several configurations and no ModelHeader. That fixes the two parts where the lowest ID was wrong:
`118a3cb0` now reads config 268 (the lowest was 56 mm off) and `289756ce` reads 21 (0.1 mm off).
When nothing matches, it keeps the lowest ID, as before.

Converter: `solidworks-converters` `dev` at b11225b (`src/parasolid/select.js`, `readBody` in
`src/parasolid/partition.js`). Scripts and outputs: `scripts/EXP098/`.

## The rule (2026-11-06)

A configuration matches when both hold:

- its B-rep, assembled as the export assembles it (first partition section, in-part LocalBodies,
  hidden bodies left out), has as many faces as the display;
- its box equals the union of the display's per-face stored bounds within 1e-8 m.

The B-rep box is every native vertex, single-vertex loops included, plus every edge's extremes
after procedural curves are fitted. Lines, circles and ellipses give exact extremes. Other curves
give 257 samples. Several matches take the lowest; EXP-094 and EXP-097 found tied configurations
export the same STEP.

Two things changed from the first version of the code, both found on `300f9732`:

- Intersection curves have to be fitted before the box (the resolver the STEP export uses).
  Without that, the box code threw and the part fell back without being measured.
- Arcs got exact extremes instead of samples. That didn't change `300f9732` (below), but samples
  bound a box from inside, so they can only cause misses.

## Why `300f9732` doesn't match (measured)

Config 0 equals the display box on five sides. On +z it's 2.5e-8 m short. The display, not the
B-rep, is off:

- The B-rep has four tolerant vertices (tolerance 1e-8 m), at y = 0.061 m and y = 0.215 m.
- The display faces at those two y values are padded: 5e-8 m in x and y, 2.5e-8 m in z. Display
  faces 47 and 55 run over the same 2 mm arcs and their top is exactly the arc's, 0.128522770956 m.
- Config 1 has 46 faces against the display's 92.

So the fallback reads config 0, which is right here because it's the only configuration with the
display's face count. The bar stays at 1e-8 m. Why the padding is 5e-8 m for a 1e-8 m tolerance is
not checked yet.

## Control: the box test on single-configuration parts (measured)

With one configuration the answer is known, so misses here are the test's false negatives.

| result | parts |
|---|---|
| match | 152 |
| box gap over 1e-8 m | 31 |
| no display bounds | 18 |
| resolver error (`2f121983`: intersection march) | 1 |
| over 180 s (`82981f2a` twice, `f666f342`) | 3 |
| total | 205 |

That's 152 of 186 measured parts, an 18% miss rate. Of the 31 gaps:

- 29 have the B-rep box inside the display box. The SW2011 sphere, torus and loft samples are
  among them: those faces bulge past their edges, and the box only looks at edges and vertices.
- 19 have tolerant vertices or edges.
- 7 are under 1 µm.
- 2 have the B-rep outside the display. `636b653d` has eight vertices 2.794 mm past the display's
  lowest y with equal face counts; `f0b9a118` (16 tolerant vertices) is 0.26 mm outside.
- `eefa906d` is the largest, 0.325 m inside, on a 6-face part.

Every miss is a false negative. On a multi-configuration part a false negative falls back to the
lowest ID, which is the old behaviour. The rule only does harm if the right configuration misses
while a wrong one matches. That didn't happen in the nine parts. It can't be ruled out in general.
