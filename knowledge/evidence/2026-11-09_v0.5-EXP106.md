# EXP-106: blind check of the one-to-one tie-breaker

The fix holds for every set tested. No pick was wrong between candidates that differ, and every
order-dependent pick was between candidates with identical geometry. The check found one silent
failure: the recursive matching overflowed the stack near 10,000 display faces. That's now fixed
(`dev` 98726a8).

A Haiku agent ran this blind against `dev` 705b69b, with its own variants of the code, sweeps and
identity checks (`scripts/EXP106/`, `findings.md`).

## Results (measured)

- `node --test test/select.test.js`: 3 of 3 pass. The old code (517476c) picks C03 for C18; 705b69b picks C18.
- SW2011 all-pairs: 25 parts, all 25 B-reps as candidates, 12 orders each. 19 of 25 pick themselves (old code 18). A variant that checks every vertex against every surface picks the same as 705b69b on all 25.
  - 15 parts have more than one fit. 12 pick themselves, 3 pick an identical twin (C22→C00, C25→C13, C26→C04), and 0 pick a different shape.
  - The twins are identical by rounded vertex set, every face surface and edge count. The control pair C04/C03 isn't.
  - 3 parts (C14 sphere, C15 torus, C16 loft) fail the box test against their own body, so the residual never runs. That's a box-test miss.
- Corpus: 9 multi-partition files, and the old code, 705b69b and the full variant agree on all 9. The 4 tied files tie between identical geometry, and their residuals agree to every digit. No real tie between different shapes exists in the corpus.

## Failure modes found

- The recursion overflowed at 10,000 faces (synthetic chain: 2,000 faces 525 ms, 5,000 faces 3.2 s). `choose()` caught the error and scored the candidate as no match, without saying so. The largest corpus display has 92 faces in a tie. Fixed in 98726a8 with breadth-first augmenting paths: 10,000 faces now take 8.8 s, results are identical to the old code up to 2,000 faces, and tests give 68 pass, 2 skipped.
- Display faces with no vertices drop out of the matching. None exist in the tied files or the SW2011 parts.

## Timing

the other 23 take under 18 ms each. Not profiled further.
